Transforming Rural Health in North Carolina: Our Progress

  1. Home
  2. Department Initiatives
  3. Rural Health Transformation Program

NCDHHS’ Rural Health Transformation Program aims to improve health in rural communities and make sure high quality care is available for years to come. 

With its first year of funding, the state is investing in proven programs while building the local and regional partnerships needed for lasting change. These investments will strengthen the rural health care workforce, improve technology and help communities connect to the care and support they need.  

The Rural Health Transformation Program is funded through the U.S. Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) for five years:

  • Year 1: North Carolina was granted $213 million from CMS. The state has awarded it to local and regional projects, as described on this page. They have until September 2027 to spend it. Read a summary of our Year 1 work.

  • Year 2: Began October 31, 2026. CMS notified states of a change in the process for awarding Year 2 program funding. All states will receive an initial $100 million award, with additional funding to follow.

Featured Projects

EMS Mobile Integrated Health (MIH) Program

$11.2 million, 40 local Emergency Medical Services agencies

The NCRHTP Mobile Integrated Health Program funds licensed North Carolina EMS agencies to bring care directly into people’s homes and connect people with the right care and resources in their communities. The program also supports the workforce needed to provide these services, improving access to behavioral health and preventive care while reducing unnecessary ambulance transports and emergency department visits. 

ROOTS Hub Leads

$123.5 million, six regions covering all NC counties 

Sets up regional networks to coordinate rural health care and direct investments based on local need. Each Hub Lead brings together providers, health departments and community partners in its region. 

Six Interconnected Initiatives

The funding is designed to support these six initiatives, plus the cost of running the program. All figures are Year 1 only. 

$0M

Community-Rooted Care Networks

Local and regional networks that connect medical care, behavioral health care and social services

$0M

Prevention and Chronic Disease Management

Better access to primary care, help managing long-term health conditions, maternal health services and nutrition support

$0M

Behavioral Health Expansion

More mental health and substance use services, including crisis care and school-based services

$0M

Workforce Development

More training and retention programs to prepare and retain people to work in rural health care

$0M

Financial Sustainability

New ways to pay health care providers that focus on the quality and results of care

$0M

Technology and Innovation

Better use of health information, digital tools and new technology

Administrative costs: Some money pays to run the program, for example staff, operations, monitoring, evaluation and fraud prevention. These expenses are strictly limited to no more than 10% of the total budget. They come out of the six initiative budgets above. In Year 1, these costs are budgeted at a total of $16.5M (7.7% of the total budget).
