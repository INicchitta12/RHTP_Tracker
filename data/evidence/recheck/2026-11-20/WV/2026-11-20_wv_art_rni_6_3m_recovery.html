Governor Morrisey Announces More Than $9.3 Million to Help West Virginians Stay in Recovery and Grow Life Science Innovation

November 19, 2026
Image

CHARLESTON, W.Va. - Governor Patrick Morrisey today announced more than $9.3 million to help West Virginians in recovery from addiction stay connected to care and to expand life science innovation in Morgantown. 

Watch the Governor’s announcement here. 

"Recovery doesn't end when someone walks out of treatment. That's when the hardest part begins," said Governor Morrisey. "Too many West Virginians fall through the cracks in those first few weeks because they can't get a ride or don't have anyone checking in. At the same time, we want the next generation of medical and life science breakthroughs to be built right here in West Virginia. These investments reach patients earlier, grow good-paying jobs in Morgantown, and help more West Virginians get healthy, get back to work, and rebuild their lives."

The package includes more than $6.3 million in West Virginia Rural Health Transformation Program (WV RHTP) awards supporting WVU's Rockefeller Neuroscience Institute (RNI). It also includes $3 million from the Office of Economic Development to the West Virginia University Innovation Corporation (WVUIC) to build shared laboratory space for startups. 

Expanding Addiction Recovery Care Through WV RHTP

The two awards were made under the State's Personal Health Accelerator and Rural Health Link initiatives. They will serve patients in 14 counties across north-central West Virginia and the Eastern Panhandle, starting at RNI's addiction care hub in Morgantown:

  • Digital Disease Management Platform ($3,000,000): Awarded to West Virginia University Hospitals, Inc., on behalf of RNI. This project expands RNI's Human Operating System (HOS), a recovery monitoring platform that links a phone app and wearable sensors to a patient's medical record. When a patient's risk of relapse rises, the platform alerts the care team so a coordinator or peer specialist can reach out early. That help can be a check-in call or help with counseling, housing, a job or transportation. The award aims to bring hundreds of new patients onto HOS in its first year, with stronger data security, privacy protections and reporting to the State.
  • Recovery Access Coordination Hub ($3,382,229): Awarded to West Virginia United Health System, on behalf of the RNI Addiction Ecosystem. This initiative will hire dozens of new staff, including recovery care coordinators, peer recovery specialists with lived experience, mobility coordinators, community outreach specialists and drivers. Coordinators will schedule each patient's first appointments before they leave treatment and help clear obstacles to care, especially transportation. The goal is to provide thousands of rides.

"The first weeks after treatment are when people are most vulnerable, and a missed appointment can be the first step toward relapse," said Secretary of Health Dr. Arvin Singh. "These two RHTP awards are built to work together. One alerts care teams when someone may need additional support, and the other puts people and transportation resources in place to respond. That's how we keep patients connected to care instead of waiting for a crisis."

"Addiction has taken a tremendous toll on West Virginia and the nation. As a chronic brain disease, it requires a comprehensive approach that expands treatment options and supports long-term recovery," said Ali Rezai, M.D., executive chair of the WVU Rockefeller Neuroscience Institute. "By integrating wearable technology, digital health, and artificial intelligence with established treatments, we can empower peer recovery support specialists and the addiction management ecosystem to provide timely and personalized support. We are grateful for Governor Morrisey's commitment to fostering health care innovation and expanding access to care. Our goal is to make West Virginia a national model for addiction treatment and recovery, improving lives and restoring hope for patients and their families."

The awards will track patient enrollment and monitoring, relapse and overdose events, rides completed and appointment completion rates. The results will measure impact and guide future recovery efforts statewide.

“President Trump’s Rural Health Transformation Program is helping states invest in innovative solutions that keep patients connected to the care and support they need,” said CMS Administrator Dr. Mehmet Oz. “By combining new technology with strong recovery teams, these investments can help more West Virginians stay in recovery, rebuild their lives, and get back to their families and communities.”

Additional RHTP awards will be announced as West Virginia continues carrying out the program to improve health outcomes, expand access to care and strengthen the health care workforce.

Growing Life Science and Energy Innovation in Morgantown

The Office of the Governor is asking the Office of Economic Development to provide $3 million to continue repurposing the former pharmaceutical manufacturing facility at 781 Chestnut Ridge Road in Morgantown. West Virginia University and the West Virginia University Health System acquired the site, and it has already attracted or created approximately 600 jobs.

  • WVUIC Incubator Labs ($3,000,000): This one-time award to WVUIC will renovate existing first-floor laboratory space into a modern, shared wet-lab incubator within 12 months. Startups in biotechnology, genomics, pharmaceuticals, medical devices and energy will gain affordable access to upgraded utilities, chemical fume hoods, biological safety cabinets, real-time PCR systems, ultra-low-temperature freezers and other specialized equipment. That access will help early-stage companies generate the data they need to win competitive National Institutes of Health and Department of Energy grants, attract private investment and create hundreds of jobs without leaving West Virginia.

 

The Rural Health Transformation Program awards in this press release are supported by the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) of the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) as part of a financial assistance award totaling $401,557,453.35 in combined Year 1 and Year 2 funding, with 100 percent funded by CMS/HHS.
