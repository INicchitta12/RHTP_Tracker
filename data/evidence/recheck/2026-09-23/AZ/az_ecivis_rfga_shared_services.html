Adopt Shared Services Consortiums

OVERVIEW 

The Arizona Health Care Cost Containment System (AHCCCS) hereby issues this Request for Grant Applications (RFGA) for the support of the Adopt Shared Services Consortiums, under the Arizona RHTP. 

This competitive grant opportunity is funded through the CMS RHTP and is intended to support the development and implementation of shared service models that reduce operational costs, eliminate duplication, and strengthen the collective capacity of rural healthcare organizations across rural and rural Tribal communities in Arizona. Funded projects will support: 

  • Shared staffing models, including joint employment arrangements, traveling clinician programs, shared administrative personnel, and locum tenens  

  • Shared training programs, including joint continuing education, simulation training centers, competency development, and cross-training  

  • Shared data systems, including joint IT infrastructure, shared billing/coding services, centralized data analytics, and common scheduling platforms  

  • Shared facilities, including co-located service hubs, shared specialty clinic space, joint equipment utilization, and collaborative care sites 

  • Joint purchasing arrangements, shared supply chain management, and equipment procurement cooperatives  

  • Shared compliance functions, including shared compliance officers, joint audit preparation, and coordinated regulatory reporting  

These investments are intended to build and strengthen collaboration among rural health facilities and other rural providers to operate more efficiently, promote the adoption of shared service models, co-located community hubs, and integrated rural networks, and enhance care coordination, reduce duplication, and promote long-term financial viability. 

BACKGROUND AND PURPOSE 

Program Background 

The Arizona RHTP is intended to strengthen healthcare access, workforce capacity, provider sustainability, and population health outcomes across rural communities throughout Arizona.  The Arizona RHTP targets approximately 800,000 rural Arizonans, representing ~11% of the State’s population, through coordinated initiatives addressing rural health workforce capacity, health care access, provider sustainability, and priority chronic disease management and prevention. 

This statewide initiative is a five-year, federally funded program designed to improve healthcare access and outcomes across rural and rural Tribal communities. Continuation funding across the five-year Period of Performance is contingent upon annual CMS review and approval of a Non-Competing Continuation (NCC) application, including applicable rescoring or continuation award recalculation conducted by CMS.    

Purpose of Adopt Shared Services Consortiums 

Within the Making Rural Healthcare Resilient Initiative, the Adopt Shared Services Consortiums sub-initiative includes funding to support the development and implementation of shared service models that reduce operational costs, eliminate duplication, and strengthen the collective capacity of rural healthcare organizations. By pooling resources and expertise, rural providers can achieve economies of scale typically available only to larger health systems. 

The Adopt Shared Services Consortiums Initiative aims to foster integration and collaboration among healthcare providers, social service organizations, and community partners to enhance care coordination, reduce duplication, and promote long-term financial viability across Arizona's rural communities. 

Specifically, this initiative aims to: 

  • Implement shared staffing, training, data systems, and facilities to improve operational efficiency and sustainability in low-population and Tribal areas  

  • Reduce overhead and strengthen rural provider networks through self-sustaining shared-service models 

  • Embed cost-sharing agreements and joint purchasing into network governance to support long-term financial viability 

  • Transition successful shared-service hubs to long-term funding through value-based payment, shared savings, and cooperative revenue models  

  • Leverage capital planning and access-to-financing technical assistance to support future growth  

  • Institutionalize shared infrastructure, including IT, billing, and HR services, beyond the grant term, aligned with AHCCCS and ADHS reimbursement modernization efforts  

The anticipated period of performance under this RFGA is from the award date through October 30, 2026, corresponding to Budget Period 1 of the Arizona RHTP.

PROJECT TYPES AND ELIGIBLE ACTIVITIES 

The Grants for Adopt Shared Services Consortiums initiative features three programmatic tiers. As part of this RFGA, applicants must propose projects that represent new or expanded activities beyond the applicant’s current operations. 

Proposed projects must demonstrate that RHTP funding will be used to: 

  • Establish new services, programs, or service delivery models; or   

  • Expand the geographic reach, population served, or service capacity of an existing program. 

Projects that solely maintain existing service levels, replace or supplant existing funding sources, or duplicate services already funded through federal, state, local, or third-party payers (including Medicaid and Medicare) are not allowed. 

Applicants must clearly describe how the proposed project differs from existing efforts and demonstrate that the proposed activities will result in a meaningful expansion in access, capacity, or service delivery. 

Tier 1 (Up to $10,000 – $95,000) 

Purpose: Enable small-scale, single-function shared service arrangements between two to three rural organizations, allowing providers to begin pooling resources at a manageable scale.  

Eligible Uses: 

  • Shared billing or coding arrangements between two to three rural organizations 

  • Joint supply procurement between neighboring rural facilities 

  • Shared compliance officer between two to three rural health organizations 

  • Common scheduling platform implemented across two to three facilities 

  • Single-function joint purchasing or group procurement cooperative 

Tier 2 (Up to $95,001 – $500,000) 

Purpose: Support the implementation of multiple shared service functions across consortiums of four to six organizations, with a formal governance structure in place to manage shared operations.  

Eligible Uses: 

  • Multi-function consortium implementing shared staffing, joint billing, and centralized data analytics 

  • Formalized regional purchasing cooperative across rural health facilities 

  • Shared administrative personnel and compliance functions across rural health clinics and FQHCs 

  • Joint continuing education and cross-training programs across a consortium of rural providers 

  • Shared data systems including joint IT infrastructure and centralized analytics platforms 

Tier 3 (Up to $500,001 – $1,500,000) 

Purpose: Support comprehensive, multi-function shared service arrangements across consortiums of seven or more organizations, including the development of regional hub models that serve as centralized infrastructure for rural health networks across a defined geographic area. 

Eligible Uses: 

  • Regional shared-service hub providing centralized IT, billing, HR, and compliance infrastructure for a network of rural hospitals, FQHCs, and clinics 

  • Comprehensive consortium implementing shared staffing, facilities, data systems, and joint purchasing across seven or more organizations 

  • Co-located community health hub delivering integrated care through shared staffing and facilities across a rural region 

  • Integrated rural health network with shared administrative and operational functions, aligned with alternative payment models and value-based care strategies  

Across all tiers, applicants are expected to demonstrate how proposed activities contribute to building and sustaining integrated rural health networks that reduce duplication, improve care coordination, and promote long-term financial viability in rural and rural Tribal communities.

Administrative Restrictions 

Under CMS guidelines, RHTP is subject to a cumulative administrative cost cap of 10% of the total award. This cap applies to the State of Arizona and all subrecipients, including all direct and indirect administrative expenditures. For the State of Arizona, this corresponds to an administrative cost allocation of $16,698,895.59 for Budget Year 1, based on the total award amount of $166,988,955.92. This limitation applies to both direct and indirect administrative expenditures.  

For Grants for the Adopt Shared Services Consortiums, AHCCCS has allocated up to 7% of each individual award for administrative costs by the grantee. The remaining 3% of the total 10% administrative cost cap has been retained by AHCCCS for program administration.  

Successful grant recipients that hold a Negotiated Indirect Cost Rate Agreement (NICRA) may apply their negotiated rate to the eligible cost categories identified in their official agreement, but the resulting indirect charges remain fully subject to the 7% administrative cap. The existence of a NICRA does not exempt any portion of administrative cost from this limit, and the 7% ceiling governs the combined total of direct and indirect administrative expenditures, consistent with the State of Arizona's RHTP guidance.
