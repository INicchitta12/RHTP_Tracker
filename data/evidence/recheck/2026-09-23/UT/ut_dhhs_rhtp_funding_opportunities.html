RISE Initiative 2.3: Grow Our Own Rural Health Pipeline 

Project overview: This initiative funds community-based programs that begin career exposure at the K-12 level and create structured pathways from high school to certification, credential, or degree in healthcare occupations. The program is designed to build a sustainable healthcare workforce in rural communities by identifying and supporting rural residents to enter healthcare careers and creating pathways that allow them to earn credentials and find employment without leaving their communities.

Funded programs must advance one or more grant objectives, including building a rural health career pipeline, creating accessible pathways to healthcare credentials, connecting completers to rural employment, strengthening workforce infrastructure, and serving high-needs populations or Tribal Nations. All funded activities must serve individuals residing or attending school in a rural Utah county including Tribal Nations and tribal communities.

SHIFT 3.3 Rural EMS Request for Grant Applications

Project overview: The Sustaining Health Infrastructure for Transformation (SHIFT) initiative is designed to transform preventive care. SHIFT focuses on enhancing local service delivery and modernizing infrastructure. It supports capital improvements, expands services like community paramedicine, and establishes integrated rural health provider networks to build a more resilient and interconnected healthcare system.

This RFGA will provide funding to rural and Tribal EMS communities to strengthen emergency medical services and workforce capacity by expanding training access, improving communications, facilitating data exchange, and enhancing community paramedicine capabilities.

Applicants must align their proposal with one or more of the following programs:

  • Program A – Education: Expand access to EMS training, reduce administrative burdens, and strengthen workforce development. 
  • Program B – Community Paramedicine & Telehealth: Expand community-based care, improve care coordination, and reduce unnecessary emergency transports. 
  • Program C – Innovation & Technology: Advance EMS capabilities through new tools, communications systems, and technology-driven training.
  • Program D – Administrative Support: Operational and administrative infrastructure required to manage, implement, and sustain grant-funded projects.

RHTP Evaluation Contract

The purpose of this RFSQ is to obtain statements of qualifications from companies to provide monitoring and evaluation services for the Utah Rural Health Transformation Program (RHTP). 

The RHTP is a five-year federally funded initiative from the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) to strengthen rural health systems. Utah’s RHTP framework is built upon four strategic pillars: Making Rural Utahns Healthy, Workforce Development, Innovation and Access, and Technology Innovation. These pillars are operationalized through 24 key activities divided into seven integrated initiatives. These initiatives and activities aim to transform rural healthcare delivery, strengthen patient-centered care, and drive measurable improvements in health, coordination, and financial sustainability across rural Utah. A network of partners including other State Agencies, community groups, private sector entities, and academia will implement the program in 25 of Utah’s 29 counties (excluding Salt Lake, Utah, Davis and Weber). 

The RHTP Monitoring and Evaluation Contract will provide critical analytical support, evaluating whether the program achieved the intended outcomes, assessing the impact of the seven initiatives, evaluating innovative pilot activities, and monitoring performance to drive course corrections to maximize impact, and prevent ongoing financial obligations for the State of Utah.

The intent of this RFSQ is to award a contract under Utah Procurement Code 63G-6a Part 15 for the purchase of Design Professional Service Providers and Consultants. The vendor with the highest- ranking will be selected to negotiate a contract at compensation determined to be fair and reasonable. If fair and reasonable compensation, contract requirements, and contract documents cannot be agreed upon with the highest ranked vendor, the state may proceed with negotiations with the next highest ranked vendor. Do NOT include pricing when you submit your qualifications. 

Estimated start date: October 15, 2026. Funding must be obligated by October 30, 2026. An executed contract constitutes a funding obligation.

 

The final day to submit questions is: September 11, 2026 at 10:30 am MDT.

Project overview: The purpose of this Request for Grant Applications (RFGA) is to transform rural healthcare access and care coordination to drive measurable improvements in health outcomes.
