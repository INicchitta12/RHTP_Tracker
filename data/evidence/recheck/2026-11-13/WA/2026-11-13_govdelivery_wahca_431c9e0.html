Washington’s RHT funds are 100% obligated

Earlier this year, the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) awarded Washington state about $181 million for year 1 of the Rural Health Transformation (RHT) Program. As of this week, these funds are 100% obligated, which means CMS legally commits to providing all the funding.

The money is now at work, reaching all 30 of Washington's rural counties and serving 1.1 million rural residents.

Where the money is

Washington’s RHT Program has six initiatives that support health care innovation in rural communities. Each was built with rural partners and approved by CMS. Here’s how year 1 funds were shared.

  • Initiative 1: Ignite innovation in rural hospitals
  • Initiative 2: Prevent disease and manage care
  • Initiative 3: Invest in the health of Native families
  • Initiative 4: Adopt technology and data solutions
  • Initiative 5: Develop Washington's rural workforce
  • Initiative 6: Expand rural behavioral health
  • Administrative costs

Administrative costs

To support running Washington’s RHT Program, including completing 144 total contracts to move funds, we've currently budgeted 5% of funding for administration. CMS allows states up 10% of the total funding for administration.

Learn more about the RHT Program

The RHT Program is part of the H.R.1 budget reconciliation bill. The program offers $50 billion in funding nationwide to improve rural health care. The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) administers the program.

In this first year, CMS awarded Washington state about $181 million. These funds will support the hospitals, clinics, and providers that rural communities rely on every day.

About the Health Care Authority (HCA)

Functioning as both the state's largest health care purchaser and its behavioral health authority, the Washington State Health Care Authority (HCA) is a leader in ensuring Washington residents have the opportunity to be as healthy as possible.

There are three pillars of our work: Apple Health (Medicaid); the Public Employees Benefits Board (PEBB) and School Employees Benefits Board (SEBB) programs; and behavioral health and recovery. Under these pillars, HCA purchases health care, including behavioral health treatment for more than 2.7 million Washington residents and provides behavioral health prevention, crisis, and recovery supports to all Washington residents.
