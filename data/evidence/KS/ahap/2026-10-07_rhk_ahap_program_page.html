Anchor Hospital Advancement Program (AHAP)

Bringing Care Closer to Home

Through the Rural Health Transformation Program (RHTP), AHAP was named as a key program to establish key locations across the state to focus on improving technology, expansion of services, access to specialty care, and enhanced patient care.

Program Focus Areas

AHAP has established priorities for year one of funding:

1. On-Demand and Consultative Resources
Make available subject matter experts to assist with location-specific needs, e.g., cybersecurity, long-term site planning and mechanical assessment, quality & safety assessments, managed care contracting
2. Artificial Intelligence and EHR optimization
Form committee to identify and prioritize operational challenges that have viable technology solutions for rapid and broad implementation
Deploy solutions across anchor hospitals to help with efficiency, patient safety, and experience
3. Remote Patient Monitoring
Deploy wearables for continuous inpatient monitoring to decrease length of stay, reduce complication rates, and reduce hospital staff workloads
4. Rural Residency Programs
Expand/develop programs in 5 high-need specialties: OB/GYN, Behavioral Health, Family Medicine, General Surgery, Psychiatry, and Orthopedics.

Identifying 10 Anchor Hospitals

Applying objective criteria, 10 rural hospitals have been designated as anchor hospitals eligible for support services and programs funded through the State’s RHT Plan. The intent is to strengthen rural access to specialty services and reduce the need for long-distance travel to urban areas. A representative from each hospital serves on the AHAP governing body, ensuring funds are deployed in an effective and efficient manner.

RURAL RESIDENCY

AHAP continues to make significant progress in developing sustainable rural residency opportunities designed to strengthen the healthcare workforce across Kansas. Through the establishment of the Office of Rural Residency (ORR), AHAP is working to expand graduate medical education (GME) capacity within the state while strengthening collaboration with academic and clinical partners.

 
Initial expansion efforts are focused on family medicine and pediatrics as well as high-need specialties including obstetrics and gynecology (OB/GYN) and psychiatry. These specialties were selected in response to critical workforce shortages across rural Kansas, where many communities have limited or no access to the care these specialties provide. Coupled with an aging physician workforce and growing demand, these residency programs are intended to improve access to care and reduce the prevalence of healthcare deserts throughout the state.
Once fully implemented, these initial programs are expected to create approximately 60 additional residency training slots and graduate roughly 20 new physicians annually. Because physicians often establish practices near where they complete their residency training, these investments represent a long-term strategy to strengthen and stabilize the rural physician workforce pipeline. Additional high-demand specialties are also being evaluated for expansion.
 
To support the successful development and sustainability of these programs, AHAP and the ORR will engage nationally recognized GME experts to help create a roadmap for long-term growth, accreditation, and operational excellence. Through these efforts, AHAP is building the infrastructure necessary to ensure rural communities have access to high-quality healthcare providers for generations to come.

 

AI EMR

AHAP has continued to advance planning for its AI-enabled EMR initiative following a comprehensive evaluation of Year 1 priority use cases. Revenue cycle management emerged as the highest-impact opportunity, with initial efforts focused on leveraging vendor solutions to enhance sustainability and drive growth through eligibility and prior authorization automation. These tools are expected to reduce administrative burden, improve efficiency, and help optimize revenue cycle performance across member organizations.

The project remains on track to achieve all Year 1 milestones by October 30, including vendor selection, contract execution, and implementation planning. As AHAP moves forward, the long-term vision is to achieve full member adoption and reach a break-even return on investment by Year 3, creating a scalable and sustainable model for continued innovation.

Following implementation kickoff, Year 2 will focus on a phased, 12-month deployment strategy, onboarding AHAP members in waves to ensure a smooth transition and provide the support needed for successful adoption. Through this collaborative approach, AHAP aims to maximize the value of AI-enabled technologies while strengthening operational efficiency and supporting the future growth of rural healthcare organizations.

RPM

Implementation planning continues at AHAP pilot sites Newman Regional Health and Labette Health as teams finalize clinical workflows and solution design for remote patient monitoring (RPM) services. The technology will help provide continuous patient monitoring and support more timely clinical interventions.

At its July board meeting, AHAP approved a recommendation for the use of a third-party monitoring provider in conjunction with BioButton® technology, marking an important milestone in the initial implementation of RPM.

Building on this progress, AHAP anticipates implementation for all anchor sites to rollout remote patient monitoring by early 2027. The expansion reflects AHAP's ongoing commitment to leveraging innovative care models that enhance patient outcomes, strengthen care coordination, and increase access to healthcare services across the region.  Additionally, the board is starting planning on bringing this program to additional rural hospitals throughout the state using RHTP funding.
