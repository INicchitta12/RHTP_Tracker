About our RHTP Awardees

The Colorado Department of Health Care Policy & Financing (HCPF) is pleased to announce the organizations selected to receive funding through Colorado’s Rural Health Transformation Program (RHTP). These awards represent a significant investment in strengthening health care access, improving health outcomes, supporting the rural health care workforce, and advancing innovative, sustainable solutions for Colorado’s rural and frontier communities. The awardees represent a diverse range of organizations, communities, and approaches to strengthening rural healthcare across Colorado. Explore the complete list of highlights of the investment, RHTP awardees, and their funding below.

Highlights of the investment include:

  • Strengthening whole-person health by better connecting physical health, behavioral health, care coordination, and community-based services.
  • Expanding telehealth and access to specialty care, including tele-ICU and telehospitalist services, remote patient monitoring, and regional telehealth networks that connect rural patients and providers with specialty expertise closer to home.
  • Building and sustaining the rural health workforce through recruitment, training, career pathways, professional development, and retention strategies.
  • Strengthening EMS and emergency care through regional training and coordination, expanded emergency and transport capacity, workforce development, and critical lifesaving equipment.
  • Expanding mobile and community-based health care services through new vehicles and mobile clinics, community paramedicine, Mobile Integrated Healthcare, and home-based services that bring care directly to rural communities.
  • Expanding behavioral health and substance use disorder care, including rural behavioral health networks, integrated behavioral and physical health services, addiction medicine, opioid use disorder treatment, crisis services, and care navigation.
  • Improving maternal, newborn, and pediatric care, including high-risk pregnancy monitoring, rural obstetric emergency readiness, maternal care navigation, postpartum and newborn home visiting, pediatric specialty care, and pediatric behavioral health.
  • Expanding lifesaving blood transfusions before patients reach the hospital by building a reliable whole-blood supply and training rural EMS providers to deliver blood to critically ill or injured patients across a network reaching 39 Colorado counties. 
  • Modernizing rural health care infrastructure and technology through electronic health record modernization, shared technology platforms, clinical equipment, digital health tools, and systems that improve care coordination and support long-term sustainability.
  • Building regional partnerships and shared-service models that allow rural hospitals, health centers, EMS agencies, behavioral health providers, colleges, and community organizations to share expertise, technology, workforce resources, and services.
