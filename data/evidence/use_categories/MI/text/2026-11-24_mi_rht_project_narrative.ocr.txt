Michigan’s Rural Description and Target Population
The state of Michigan is well known for the nation's longest freshwater coastline surrounded by
the Great Lakes, more than 11,000 inland lakes and two separate peninsulas. Though these
features can be enjoyed by Michigan’s 10 million residents, they also pose unique challenges to
the delivery of health care, particularly in rural areas. With 75 of its 83 counties classified as
rural in whole or in part and more than 20% of the Michigan residents living in rural areas,
Michigan has the seventh largest rural population in the nation.
One unique aspect of Michigan’s geography is the Upper Peninsula, where health care access
challenges are exacerbated by the vast land size and low population density. This challenge is
not exclusive to the Upper Peninsula, Michigan’s rural areas in the Lower Peninsula are also
characterized with rugged forests, agricultural land, and sparce population. These features result
in long travel distances and limited access to many routine and specialized health care services
and resources. Michigan also has several habitable islands dotting the coastline. These islands
have a mix of seasonal and year-round residents which makes high-quality and accessible
healthcare arrangements a need on each island.

income lags the U.S. average by over $15,000, with all rural counties in Michigan falling below

Defining Rural Areas in Michigan
Michigan defines rural areas to include 75 counties that are either fully rural or partially rural
counties as identified by the Federal Office of Rural Health Policy within Health Resources and
Services Administration (HRSA). This program will prioritize counties that experience high
chronic disease prevalence; limited access to hospitals, primary care, OB/GYN, and behavioral
health services; high Medicaid dependence and socioeconomic vulnerability; and significant
rates of child poverty or aging populations. For the purposes of the Rural Health Transformation

Target Population
Michigan’s proposal targets a subset of 75 rural or partially rural counties statewide. This target
allows the inclusion of counties with significant rural census tracts. These counties typically lag
in health infrastructure, provider availability, and socioeconomic resources, making them prime
candidates for transformative interventions. Our target population includes rural residents,
Medicaid beneficiaries, older adults and children and families experiencing poverty or adverse
health outcomes in these high-need counties. We will prioritize counties that exhibit high chronic
disease prevalence, limited access to hospitals, primary care, maternal health, behavioral health
services, and high Medicaid utilization. By concentrating resources in these 75 counties, this
program aims to stabilize rural hospitals and clinics, expand access to primary, maternal, and
behavioral health services, and support sustainable care models where gaps are most acute.
Rural health transformation plan: Goals and strategies
Michigan’s vision is to create a resilient and innovative rural health system where every resident
has access to high-quality care close to home. By leveraging partnerships, workforce
development, technology, and community-centered care models, the state will improve health
outcomes, expand access, and ensure sustainable rural health infrastructure by fiscal year
(FY)2031.

Description: The Transforming Rural Health through Partnerships Initiative is designed to
reimagine Michigan’s rural health landscape by testing and scaling new, community-driven
approaches to care. Michigan will develop Hubs that function as a collaborative network of
local, community, and regional partners that work together to improve health and social
outcomes. Hubs will prioritize the utilization of CHWs, leverage CIE and broader HIE systems,
and integrate Health in All Policies (HiAP) approaches to address the root causes of disease. By
fostering interdisciplinary partnerships across health care, behavioral health, and social services,
Hubs create sustainable, coordinated solutions that strengthen community capacity, enhance care
delivery, and improve health outcomes.
This Initiative introduces fresh models of CHW integration, interdisciplinary chronic disease
management, and behavioral health workflows, while also pioneering financing strategies that
enable long-term sustainability. By piloting innovative solutions with local health departments,
FQHCs, school-based clinics, rural health clinics, hospitals, and community-based organizations,

new pathways to improving outcomes.

department visits, strengthening local care systems, and ensuring that every resident can receive
high-quality care close to home. The Care Closer to Home Blueprint Initiative also includes
complementary components to the other three overarching initiatives (Transforming Rural

care access improvements with strengthened partnerships, workforce development, and data
connectivity to create a fully integrated rural health ecosystem. The Care Closer to Home

Michigan’s Rural Health Transformation Program is designed to create lasting improvements in
rural health care delivery, workforce capacity, and infrastructure. Initiatives such as telehealth
programs, home-based care services, and workforce development programs will be fully
integrated into MDHHS operational policies and procedures, ensuring that these efforts persist
beyond the conclusion of the Rural Health Transformation funding in FY31. By embedding these

With Medicaid’s expansive telehealth coverage as a foundation, additional proven and

potential permanent inclusion as covered services. MDHHS will explore legislative

that high-impact programs, particularly those that expand access to primary care, behavioral

Medicaid benefits and policy priorities.
The partnerships established through the Rural Health Transformation Program are being
structured with sustainability in mind. Regional collaboratives that generate savings will have
mechanisms to reinvest those resources into continued operations. These partnerships will
formalize governance structures, decision-making processes, and performance metrics to
promote alignment, accountability, and ongoing commitment from participating entities. Early,
frequent, and ongoing stakeholder engagement, including advisory boards, private-sector
partners, and interagency collaboration through the Medicaid Advisory Council (MAC) and
Beneficiary Advisory Council (BAC), will ensure that community voices shape decisions and
that accountability for permanent change is shared across sectors.
