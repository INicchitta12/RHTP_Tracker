Michigan Rural Health Transformation Annual Report

Note: MDHHS submitted its Year 1 annual report to the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) in accordance with applicable
reporting requirements. CMS has accepted the report as submitted. To support transparency, MDHHS is making the report publicly available
and sharing it with the Michigan Legislature, with the understanding that certain information may be subject to further review, clariﬁcation,
or reﬁnement as CMS continues to work with states.

Initiative 1: Transforming Rural Health through Partnerships

         #                                                                         Initiative Name

         1                                                         Transforming Rural Health through Partnerships

                                                                               Narrative


    The Transforming Rural Health through Partnerships Initiative builds and formalizes cross-sector partnerships among healthcare providers, public health agencies,
      community-based organizations, and regional stakeholders to improve access to integrated care in rural communities. The initiative expands coordinated care
    models, strengthens chronic disease management and preventive services, supports community health worker integration, and leverages data-sharing and referral
                                   infrastructure to improve health outcomes and long-term sustainability of rural healthcare delivery.

       Budget Period 1 established the foundation for community-driven rural health partnerships focused on chronic disease management, CHW integration, and
    coordinated care projects. MDHHS allocated approximately $26.0M to this pillar and received 75 LOIs totaling approximately $23.0M by July 2026. One competitive
      GFO was released for chronic disease programming ($2.5M). Activities focused on standing up regional hubs, strengthening partnerships among FQHCs, rural
    hospitals, CAHCs, family resource centers, and community-based organizations, and preparing cross-sector networks to improve access to preventive, behavioral
                                                                health, and chronic disease services.

Initiative 2: Workforce for Wellness

      #                                                                          Initiative Name

      2                                                                      Workforce for Wellness

                                                                             Narrative

  The Workforce for Wellness Initiative strengthens the rural health workforce through recruitment, training, retention, and workforce development strategies. The
   initiative supports educational pathways, workforce incentives, provider recruitment and retention efforts, and community health worker capacity building to
  address workforce shortages and improve access to primary care, behavioral health, maternal health, and other essential healthcare services in rural Michigan.

 Budget Period 1 focused on developing Michigan's rural workforce pipeline through provider recruitment, retention, training, and educational pathway investments.
     MDHHS allocated approximately $44.7M to this pillar and received 18 LOIs totaling approximately $4.5M by July 2026. Five workforce-related GFOs totaling
  approximately $34.2M were released, supporting recruitment and retention programs, maternal health workforce development, EMS workforce initiatives, MSW
    training pathways, and health care career pipelines. Activities emphasized long-term workforce sustainability and addressing shortages in behavioral health,
                                                          maternal health, EMS, and primary care services.

Initiative 3: Interoperability in Action

      #                                                                            Initiative Name

      3                                                                        Interoperability in Action

                                                                               Narrative

 The Interoperability in Action Initiative enhances healthcare data exchange and interoperability across rural Michigan by strengthening health information exchange
   capabilities, community information exchange infrastructure, telehealth connectivity, and data integration. The initiative supports coordinated care, improved
  access to information, reduced administrative burden, and expanded technology adoption to improve care delivery, population health management, and patient
                                                                    outcomes in rural communities.

       Budget Period 1 activities focused on expanding health information exchange, digital infrastructure, and rural provider connectivity. MDHHS allocated
  approximately $48.8M to this pillar and received 20 LOIs totaling approximately $16.8M by July 2026. No competitive GFOs had been released during this period;
  implementation primarily occurred through targeted LOIs and interagency partnerships. Activities included planning for interoperability governance, supporting
   rural provider participation in HIE/CIE systems, modernizing health technology infrastructure, and developing solutions to improve care coordination and data
                                                                     sharing across rural settings.

Initiative 4: Care Closer to Home Blueprint

      #                                                                          Initiative Name

      4                                                                   Care Closer to Home Blueprint

                                                                             Narrative

  The Care Closer to Home Initiative expands community-based models of care that allow rural residents to receive services closer to where they live. The initiative
 promotes home- and community-based care, strengthens provider and community partnerships through hub-and-spoke models, improves transportation access,
               and expands access to behavioral health services to reduce barriers to care and improve health outcomes across rural communities.

  Budget Period 1 focused on expanding access to services through transportation, behavioral health innovation, aging supports, and hub-and-spoke care delivery
  models. MDHHS allocated approximately $41.7M to this pillar and received 17 LOIs totaling approximately $18.5M by July 2026. One transportation-related GFO
    totaling approximately $12.0M was released. Activities emphasized reducing barriers to care, strengthening community-based service delivery, developing
                     transportation solutions, advancing behavioral health access, and supporting aging-in-place strategies for rural residents.

                                                                            Success Stories

                         Please share success stories that you want to highlight as result of your State’s implementation of the RHT Program.


  MDHHS advanced from program award to governance establishment, outreach to 252 organizations, 143 executed LOIs totaling $73.5M, release of seven GFOs
                            totaling $48.7M, and a successful CMS site visit within the first seven months of implementation.




                                                                         Sustainability Planning

           What are the most significant updates or changes to your sustainability plan based on the past year's experiences, successes, and challenges?


    Sustainability planning during the reporting period focused on establishing long-term program infrastructure, governance, and financing mechanisms that will
      support continuity beyond the federal funding period. MDHHS established an Advisory Council, formalized governance structures across MDHHS, MPHI,
 Guidehouse, Tribal Partners, and other stakeholders, and developed five-year implementation and annual work plans to support long-term program management.
  The State also structured investments through a combination of Letters of Intent, Grant Funding Opportunities, and Interagency Agreements designed to promote
 statewide reach, accountability, and long-term partnership development. Technical assistance strategies, program monitoring functions, evaluation activities, and
                       data infrastructure planning were initiated to support continuous improvement and inform future sustainability decisions.




                                                                            Rural Definition

                    Please provide the definition of 'rural area' that the State used to determine whether subrecipients were considered to be rural.


    Michigan defines rural areas to include 75 counties that are either fully rural or partially rural counties as identified by the Federal Office of Rural Health Policy
 (FORHP) within the Health Resources and Services Administration (HRSA). The program prioritizes counties with high chronic disease prevalence, limited access to
  hospitals, primary care, OB/GYN, and behavioral health services, high Medicaid dependence, socioeconomic vulnerability, and significant rates of child poverty or
                                                                            aging populations.

Checkpoint 0.2: Submit project narrative to CMS

                                                                 Checkpoint Narrative




 The RHTP Project Management Plan serves as the primary tool for organizing, coordinating, and monitoring implementation across each of
 Michigan’s four RHTP initiatives. The plan translates initiative-level goals into defined stages, projects, and milestones, providing a structured view
 of the work required to move each initiative from planning through implementation and completion.

 For each milestone, the plan identifies anticipated start and target completion dates to support sequencing, identified in the project narrative by
 fiscal year. Notes are included as needed to document key considerations, dependencies, decisions, risks, or other information relevant to
 implementation and ongoing monitoring.

 The plan is intended to be a living management tool and will be updated as implementation progresses, milestones are achieved, timelines or
 dependencies change, and additional actions are identified. Quarterly review of the plan will support progress monitoring, early identification of
 implementation risks or delays, coordination across RHTP initiatives, and alignment with overall program objectives and requirements.
