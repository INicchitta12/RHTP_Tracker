Use of Funds Guidance
March 2026


          OVERVIEW
          Nevada’s Rural Health Transformation (RHT) Program is designed to truly transform healthcare in rural
          Nevada by supporting innovative projects that improve access, quality, and sustainability. This document
          outlines the allowable uses of RHT dollars so potential subrecipients and vendors/contractors may
          understand clearly how funds can be applied to advance rural health transformation efforts. This
          document should serve as a guide to help applicants align proposed activities with program goals and
          ensure compliance with state and federal requirements.

          The terms and conditions of federal awards will flow down to subawards, subrecipients, and contract
          vendors per relevant cost limitations, and all contracts will be consistent with the requirements specified
          by the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS) and the state’s approved RHT application.


          RURAL HEALTH TRANSFORMATION GRANT LIMITATIONS
          The RHT funds have specific limitations outside of normal federal guidance under 2 Code of
          Federal Regulations (CFR) Part 200, outlined below. The following are unallowable costs under
          the RHT Program:
                •    New construction is unallowable. Supplanting funding for in-process or planned construction
                     projects or directing funding towards new construction builds is unallowable. Renovations or
                     alterations may be allowed if they are clearly linked to program goals and receive CMS approval.
                     Renovation or alterations funding cannot exceed 20% of the total funding CMS awards States in
                     each budget period.
                •    To replace payment for clinical services that could be reimbursed by insurance. We will not accept
                     payments to clinical services if they duplicate billable services and/or attempt to change payment
                     amounts of existing fee schedules. If you plan to fund direct health care services, you must justify
                     why they are not already reimbursable, how the payment will fill a gap in care coverage (such as
                     uncompensated care or services not covered by insurance), and/or how they transform the
                     current care delivery model. Funding for allowable provider payments cannot exceed 15% of the
                     total funding CMS awards States in a given budget period.
                •    Funding cannot be used for initiatives that fund certain cosmetic and experimental procedures
                     that fall within the definition of a specified sex-trait modification procedure at 45 CFR 156.400
                     because that is beyond the scope of this program.
                •    No more than 5% of total funding CMS awards to a State in a given budget period can support
                     funding the replacement of an EMR system if a previous HITECH certified EMR system is already
                     in place as of September 1, 2025.
                •    Funding towards technology initiatives are limited to $20M of total funding awarded to a State in
                     a given budget period.
                •    Funds may not be used for clinician salaries. Funds also may not be used for clinician salaries or
                     wage supports for facilities that subject clinicians to non-compete contractual limitations.
                •    Indirect and administrative funds are limited to 10% for all sub-recipients.

FEDERAL FUNDING POLICIES AND LIMITATIONS

Changes in U.S. Department of Health & Human Services (HHS) Regulations - Awards will
be subject to any applicable provisions of 2 CFR Part 200 and 2 CFR Part 300. As of October 1, 2025, HHS
will adopt 2 CFR Part 200, with some modifications included in 2 CFR Part 300. These regulations can be
found at 89 FR 80055 and replace those in 45 CFR Part 75.

The following costs are also unallowable under the RHT Program:
     •    Pre-award costs.
     •    Meeting match requirements for any other federal funds or local entities.
     •    Services, equipment, or supports that are the legal responsibility of another party under federal,
          State, or tribal law, such as vocational rehabilitation or education services.
     •    Services, equipment, or supports that are the legal responsibility of another party under any civil
          rights law, such as modifying a workplace or providing accommodations that are obligations
          under law.
     •    Goods or services not allocable to the project.
     •    Supplanting existing State, local, tribal, or private funding of infrastructure or services, such as
          staff salaries.
     •    Construction or building expansion, purchasing or significant retrofitting of buildings, cosmetic
          upgrades, or any other cost that materially increases the value of the capital or useful life as a
          direct cost.
     •    The cost of independent research and development, including their proportionate share of
          indirect costs. See 2 CFR 300.477.
     •    Funds related to any activity designed to influence the enactment of legislation, appropriations,
          regulation, administrative action, or executive order.
     •    Purchase of covered telecommunications and video surveillance equipment (See 2 CFR 200.216)
          as well as financial assistance to households for installation and monthly broadband internet
          costs.
     •    Meals, unless in limited circumstances such as: Subjects and patients under study.
              o Where specifically approved as part of the project or program activity, such as in
                   programs providing children’s services.
              o As part of a per diem or subsistence allowance provided in conjunction with allowable
                   travel.
     •    Activities prohibited under 2 CFR 200.450 and the HHS Grants Policy Statement, including but
          not limited to: Paying the salary or expenses of any grant recipient, or agent acting for such
          recipient, related to any activity designed to influence the enactment of legislation,
          appropriations, regulation, administrative action, or executive order proposed or pending
          before the Congress or any State government, State legislature, or local legislature or legislative
          body.
     •    Lobbying, but awardees can lobby at their own expense if they can segregate federal funds from
          other financial resources used for lobbying.
     •    For guidance on some types of costs that are restricted or not allowed, see 2 CFR Part 200
          Subpart E - General Provisions for Selected Items of Cost.
