Section 1: Program Description & Purpose
As Idaho’s population ages, Alzheimer’s disease and other dementias (ADOD) increasingly affect individuals, families, healthcare systems, and communities.  Early identification of cognitive concerns can improve care planning, prolong patient independence, reduce avoidable healthcare utilization, and connect individuals and family caregivers to available resources sooner. To connect rural communities, services can be provided face-to-face or through telehealth services. 
Many rural healthcare providers serve as the primary point of contact for older adults experiencing cognitive changes but may face barriers including workforce shortages, limited specialty access, competing clinical demands across the healthcare system, and inconsistent processes for cognitive assessment and follow-up. 
Establishing structured cognitive detection and care coordination pathways within rural healthcare settings can help providers identify concerns earlier, standardize referral processes, improve care planning, and strengthen connections between healthcare systems, family caregivers, and community-based supports.
The Department seeks up to two (2) subgrantees to create and implement the Idaho Cognitive Care Pathway Network by increasing the adoption of evidence-based cognitive detection and care coordination models that: 
strengthen coordination among healthcare providers, family caregivers, and community-based organizations; 
equip rural healthcare providers to identify cognitive impairment and facilitate appropriate follow-up; and 
improve access to services that promote health, independence, and quality of life for Idahoans. 
Applicants must be capable of administering and coordinating a statewide or multi-regional network of subgrantees and ensure a broad geographic reach. Applicants should demonstrate their ability to facilitate local implementation through partnerships, subawards, technical assistance, training, coordination, and oversight activities. 
The Idaho Cognitive Care Pathway Network must collaborate with Idaho's existing ADOD service network, including the Alzheimer’s Disease and Related Dementias Program (ADRD) and Alliance, Family Caregiver Navigator Program, and Idaho Community Care Program, to ensure individuals living with Alzheimer’s disease and other dementias and their family caregivers receive coordinated access to education, support services, care planning assistance, and community-based resources.

1.3 Program Goals & Objectives
The overarching goal of this federal award is to improve access to timely, coordinated, person-centered cognitive health services for Idahoans living in rural and underserved communities by strengthening early detection, referral, care coordination, and caregiver engagement across healthcare and community systems.
This solicitation aligns with Idaho's Rural Health Transformation initiative #4 (Implementing population specific, evidence-based projects to Make America Healthy Again) which focuses on strengthening rural healthcare delivery, care coordination, and access to services for aging populations and individuals experiencing cognitive impairment. Informed by data collected through the ADRD Program and guided by the 2023–2028 ADRD Statewide Strategic Plan, the following goals have been identified.
Goal 1: Increase Early Identification of Cognitive Concerns
Implement standardized cognitive detection processes in participating healthcare settings to improve identification of individuals who may benefit from further evaluation, care planning, or support services.
Goal 2: Strengthen Rural Provider Capacity
Train healthcare providers to integrate cognitive assessment and follow-up activities into routine clinical workflows and improving confidence in responding to cognitive concerns.
Goal 3: Improve Care Coordination and Referral Pathways
Establish or enhance referral and care coordination pathways connecting healthcare providers, specialists, community-based organizations, and dementia-related support services.
Goal 4: Engage Family Caregivers and Care Partners
Incorporate family caregivers and care partners into care planning and referral processes and facilitate connections to family caregiver  resources when appropriate.
Goal 5: Expand Access Through Flexible Service Delivery
Increase access to cognitive detection and care coordination services through in-person, telehealth, or hybrid approaches, particularly in rural and underserved communities.
Goal 6: Generate Actionable Data and Continuous Improvement
Track program activities, referrals, care coordination services, and available outcome measures to inform program improvement and statewide planning.
RHT Initiative Alignment
This solicitation advances the following Rural Health Transformation priorities:
Strengthening rural healthcare delivery systems.
Improving care coordination across providers and community services.
Expanding access to services for aging populations.
Developing, enhancing, and sustaining earlier intervention and management of chronic and cognitive health conditions.
Reducing barriers to care in rural and underserved communities.
1.4 Scope of Work
Required Activities:
Complete the Non-Duplication Assessment (Appendix H) and submit it with the application to ensure proposed activities do not duplicate existing programs, services, or projects.
Develop and implement an evidence-informed, Idaho-specific clinical follow-up pathway for individuals in rural Idaho with suspected cognitive impairment by building upon existing Idaho initiatives, available state data, lessons learned, reviewing existing national evidence-based protocols, engaging Idaho healthcare stakeholders, adapting best practices in Idaho’s healthcare delivery system, and piloting one or two selected pathways to assess feasibility, effectiveness, and scalability. 
Develop and administer a subgrant program that implements standardized, evidence-informed Alzheimer's disease and other dementias (ADOD) detection and follow-up protocols within participating healthcare and community-based settings serving rural Idahoans.
Provide technical assistance, training, and implementation guidance to subgrantees to strengthen incorporation of cognitive assessment activities into routine clinical and community-based workflows when appropriate, while promoting consistency with existing Department initiatives, resources, and statewide practices.
Provide technical assistance for subgrantees establishing procedures for identifying individuals in rural Idaho who may benefit from additional cognitive evaluation, care planning, or support services.
Develop, enhance, and sustain referral and care coordination pathways that connect healthcare providers, specialists, community-based organizations, and ADOD-related services across participating service areas.
Coordinate with the Department, the ADRD Alliance, the legislatively established ADRD Workgroup, and other statewide and regional partners to strengthen referral networks, incorporate existing statewide knowledge and recommendations, and promote consistent implementation across participating subgrantee sites.
Collaborate with the Department to incorporate existing statewide cognitive health initiatives, implementation experience, available data, and lessons learned into project planning, development, and implementation activities.
Ensure subgrantees provide care coordination activities for individuals in rural Idaho identified through ADOD detection and screening efforts.
Establish processes to refer eligible individuals and family caregivers in rural Idaho to local Area Agencies on Aging, the Idaho Community Care Program, and other appropriate programs for resource navigation, caregiver education, and support services.
Collect, monitor, and report data from subgrantees related to ADOD detection activities, referrals, care coordination efforts, and program outcomes when available.
Ensure subgrantees engage family caregivers and care partners in rural Idaho and facilitate referrals to the Idaho Community Care Program, Family Caregiver Navigator Program, and other relevant services when appropriate.
Promote and facilitate connections between participating subgrantees, educational programs, evidence-based interventions, support groups, respite resources, and other health-related services available through community organizations and ADRD Alliance partners.
Provide oversight, coordination, and implementation guidance to ensure care coordination activities are implemented consistently across participating subgrantee organizations and aligned with existing statewide initiatives and Department priorities.
Submit required programmatic, financial, and performance reports to the Department.
Maintain documentation of subgrant activities, services delivered, referrals issued, technical assistance provided, and program outcomes.
Participate in Department-required evaluation, monitoring, quality improvement, and continuous learning activities, including facilitating participation by subgrantees as requested.
The Subgrantee must meet at least quarterly with the Department’s Rural Health Transformation Program Data Analytics and Evaluation Contractor to complete the following tasks:  
Develop theory of change diagram(s) (e.g., logic model, line-of-sight diagram, driver diagram, or similar visualization of assumptions connecting proposed work with intended outcomes and performance indicators) 
Use data sharing mechanisms (to be developed by the Data Analytics and Evaluation contractor) to report on required key performance objectives (KPO) within timelines established by the Department.
Develop and implement a process evaluation and quality improvement plan that identifies useful evaluation or process improvement measures in addition to required KPOs. 
Review and provide input on draft evaluation findings, reports, and analyses as requested. 
Measures to be defined in consultation with the Data Analytics and Evaluation contractor after award.
1.5 Target Population & Service Area
Eligible service areas are rural and frontier counties within the state of Idaho, as defined by state rural health criteria and federal rural-urban classification systems, including non-metropolitan counties and rural census tracts designated under USDA Rural-Urban Continuum Codes (RUCC) or the Federal Office of Rural Health Policy (FORHP) eligibility criteria. 
See Appendices A and B for the RHTP eligibility map and a list of eligible and ineligible cities by county.
The ultimate beneficiaries of this solicitation include adults living within communities meeting the above definition who may be experiencing cognitive decline, Alzheimer’s disease or other dementias, or other cognitive concerns, including:
Individuals who are at increased risk for cognitive impairment due to age, chronic disease, disability, or other health-related factors.
Family caregivers, care partners, and people involved in the care of individuals experiencing cognitive decline.
Healthcare providers and care teams serving adults in rural communities who require enhanced systems and processes for ADOD-related detection, referral, and care coordination.
Services developed and funded through this solicitation must benefit rural and frontier communities across Idaho where access to cognitive health services, specialty care, and care coordination resources may be limited.
Applicants must propose a strategy that ensures broad geographic reach and equitable access across rural Idaho. Proposed approaches may include:
Administration of subawards to healthcare providers, community-based organizations, nonprofits, public health districts, or other eligible entities. 
Regional or multi-county implementation networks. 
Statewide coordination models that strengthen local service delivery and referral pathways. 
Partnerships that strengthen access to cognitive detection, care coordination, caregiver support, and related services. 
Applicants should clearly describe how their proposed structure will facilitate implementation across rural and frontier Idaho and improve access to services for Idahoans living with cognitive concerns and their family caregivers.
Priority Populations:
Consistent with the goals of the Rural Health Transformation initiative, priority populations include:
Older adults residing in rural and frontier communities. 
Individuals experiencing cognitive impairment, dementia, or suspected cognitive decline who face barriers to timely identification, evaluation, or support. 
Individuals with limited access to specialty healthcare services due to geographic distance, transportation challenges, workforce shortages, or other barriers to care. 
Family caregivers and care partners who need information, education, resources, or assistance navigating healthcare and community services. 
Rural healthcare providers and healthcare organizations seeking to strengthen their ability to identify cognitive concerns, coordinate care, and connect individuals and families to available services. 
Applicants are encouraged to demonstrate how proposed activities will improve access to cognitive health services, strengthen early identification and care coordination, and improve outcomes for Idahoans living in rural and frontier communities.

5.2 Review Process
The review process consists of three stages:
Stage 1 – Administrative Screening: Department staff will screen applications for completeness and eligibility. Incomplete applications or those from ineligible organizations will not advance to technical review. Applicants will be notified of disqualification within 5 business days.
Stage 2 – Technical Review: A review panel of at least three individuals with expertise in cognitive health, dementia care, healthcare delivery, care coordination, public health program management, and federal grants compliance. Reviewers will participate in a consensus scoring session to discuss and finalize scores. Panel members will sign conflict of interest disclosures.
Stage 3 – Final Selection: The department will make final funding decisions based on panel scores, geographic coverage across the state, population reach, and programmatic priorities. The Department reserves the right to fund applications out of rank order to ensure equitable geographic distribution.
The Department may request clarification, additional information, or interviews from applicants as part of the review process. Funding recommendations will be developed based on final evaluation scores, risk assessment results, available funding, and the Department’s goal of establishing an effective statewide Idaho Cognitive Care Pathway Network. The Department anticipates funding one (1) to two (2) Lead Entities capable of administering and overseeing a statewide network of local implementation partners.
Final funding decisions will consider:
Application scores

Geographic coverage
Organizational capacity
Program priorities
Ability to achieve statewide population-level outcomes

Section 8: Post Award Anticipated Timeline
Following award execution, Lead Entities will be expected to complete the following activities and deliverables in phases.
Phase 1: Startup and Network Development (Months 1–3)
Phase 2: Partner Selection and Pathway Implementation Planning (Months 3–6)
Phase 3: Cognitive Care Pathway Implementation (Months 6–24)
Phase 4: Sustainability and Project Closeout (Final 6 Months of Performance Period)
Ongoing Requirements Throughout the Project Period
Lead Entities shall maintain ongoing compliance with all reporting, monitoring, and performance requirements throughout the project period, including:
Monthly reimbursement requests
Quarterly financial reports
Quarterly programmatic reports
Quarterly performance measure reporting
Monitoring of implementation partners and subgrantees
Participation in Department-led meetings and evaluation activities
Continuous quality improvement activities
Maintenance of statewide referral and care coordination network documentation
Ongoing technical assistance to participating organizations
The Department reserves the right to modify timelines, reporting schedules, or milestone expectations as necessary to ensure successful implementation of the Idaho Cognitive Care Pathway Network.
