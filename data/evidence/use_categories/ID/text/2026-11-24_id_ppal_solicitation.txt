Section 1: Program Description & Purpose


1.1 Background
The Department will serve as the lead agency for administering the Rural Health Transformation
Program (RHTP) Grant funding award for the state of Idaho (ID). As such, the Department is
committed to removing barriers to treatment, improving health outcomes, and promoting patient-
centered care. The RHTP website address is: https://healthandwelfare.idaho.gov/providers/rural-
health-transformation-program-grant/about-rural-health-transformation-program-grant
As the grant recipient, the Department manages the entire grant award and is responsible for
distributing funding to other ID state agencies, contractors, and subrecipients in alignment with the
projects awarded by Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS). This distribution will be
carried out in accordance with both state and federal regulation, specifically procurement rules, to
facilitate the execution of RHTP work. The state’s RHTP is a critical mechanism to advance a
sustainable, data-driven model of care that enhances access, quality, and outcomes.
Idaho’s RHTP (Attachment A, ID RHTP Project Narrative) is a multi-year strategic plan including five
(5) major initiatives designed to strengthen rural health systems, increase access to care, enhance
quality outcomes, expand workforce capacity, and promote financial sustainability. These initiatives
address CMS’ strategic goals for this grant to transform healthcare for residents in the state’s rural
areas.
The five initiatives are:
Initiative 1. Improving rural access to care through technology
     • Facility technology assessments and shared infrastructure
     • Telehealth expansion
     • Digital health
     • Cybersecurity modernization and artificial intelligence (AI) tools
     • Emergency communication systems implementation and enrollment
     • Health management and data analytics tools
     • EHR software and upgrades
Initiative 2. Ensuring accessible, quality care through innovative models
     • Diagnostic and care access innovations
     • Health extenders
     • Emergency medical service (EMS) expansion and workforce stabilization
     • Understand opportunities to leverage health extenders and optimize service delivery
Initiative 3. Sustaining rural workforce with training, recruitment, and retention.
    •   Ladder payments based on priority positions and rural presence
    •   Healthcare career exploration and advancement programs
    •   Work-based learning
    •   Graduate medical education (GME) programs
Initiative 4. Implementing population specific, evidence-based projects to Make America Healthy
Again
     • Chronic disease prevention and treatment
     • Behavioral health prevention and treatment
     • Maternal and child health efforts
Initiative 5. Investing in rural health infrastructure and partnerships
    •   Healthcare facility renovations
    •   Pharmacy solutions
    •   Clinical equipment purchases
    •   Vehicles for patient transport and rural mobile health units
    •   Healthcare facility federal, state, and local safety code compliance
    •   Tribal rural health transformation support

The state’s RHTP leverages partnerships with state and local agencies, tribal partners, healthcare
providers, and community organizations to ensure locally responsive solutions.
This program will work to modernize electronic and logistical infrastructure, strengthen community-
to-clinician connections, and further integrate healthcare and community-based services across
settings and types of care.

This solicitation supports Initiative 4: Implementing Population Specific, Evidence-Based Projects to
Make America Healthy Again, specifically behavioral health prevention and treatment and maternal
and child health efforts.

The Idaho Department of Health and Welfare (DHW), Division of Behavioral Health (DBH), is seeking
to establish a statewide Idaho Pediatric Psychiatry Access Line (PAL) through partnership with one of
Idaho’s public universities, consistent with Idaho Code §67-2332A and interagency contracting
requirements.

Idaho faces significant gaps in pediatric behavioral health care due to workforce shortages,
geographic disparities, and long wait times for psychiatric services. The Pediatric Psychiatry Access
Line (PAL) is intended to function as a free, statewide resource to support primary care providers
(PCPs) in managing youth behavioral and mental health concerns through expert consultation,
education, referral coordination, and data-informed system improvement.
The selected university partner will collaborate with DHW to design, implement, and operate a
Pediatric Psychiatry Access Line that includes both a Warm Access Line consultation model and an
integrated continuing education component.

1.3 Program Goals & Objectives
The goal of this solicitation is to establish and sustain a statewide Pediatric Psychiatry Access Line
that increases access to pediatric behavioral health expertise and supports Idaho primary care
providers in managing pediatric mental and behavioral health concerns.

Specific objectives include:

    1. Establish and implement a statewide Pediatric Psychiatry Access Line within the first year of
       award.

    2. Increase access to child and adolescent psychiatric consultation for Idaho primary care
       providers.

    3. Improve provider confidence and capacity to diagnose and manage pediatric behavioral
       health conditions.

    4. Improve referral coordination and access to behavioral health services for Idaho youth and
       families.

    5. Develop and deliver a statewide pediatric behavioral health educational series with
       emphasis on rural and underserved communities.

    6. Collect and evaluate program data to support quality improvement, system planning, and
       sustainability.

1.4 Scope of Work
The selected applicant must implement and operate a statewide Pediatric Psychiatry Access Line
(PAL) program that includes the following required activities:
Required Activities
Warm Access Line Consultation Service
    •   Establish and maintain a dedicated consultation line staffed by child/adolescent
        psychiatrists and/or qualified behavioral health professionals.
    •   Implement a rapid response consultation framework with consultations scheduled within
        twenty-four (24) to forty-eight (48) hours of provider request.
   •   Provide consultation support related to diagnostic clarification, medication management, and
       case consultation for Idaho primary care providers.
   •   Provide integrated referral coordination to available behavioral health services statewide.
Provider Onboarding and Training
   •   Develop and implement a required onboarding and educational module for providers prior to
       participation in the consultation service.
   •   Establish standardized clinical language, consultation expectations, and documentation
       requirements.
Pediatric Behavioral Health Educational Series
   •   Develop and deliver a pediatric behavioral health educational series informed by
       consultation trends.
   •   Provide continuing education credits when applicable.
   •   Ensure statewide access to training opportunities with emphasis on rural and underserved
       providers.
Data Collection and Evaluation
   •   Track consultation volume, consultation response time, provider participation, referral
       outcomes, provider satisfaction, and case complexity.
   •   Conduct provider satisfaction surveys, pre- and post-assessments, and other evaluation
       activities.
   •   Use data to identify systemic barriers and support future sustainability and funding
       opportunities.
Program Administration and Sustainability
   •   Maintain staffing and infrastructure necessary to operate the program.
   •   Coordinate with DHW on reporting, monitoring, evaluation, and performance management.
   •   Support development of sustainability strategies and long-term funding approaches.


1.5 Target Population & Service Area
Target Population
The target population for this project includes Idaho primary care providers serving pediatric
populations, including physicians, nurse practitioners, physician assistants, and other licensed
healthcare providers.
Indirect beneficiaries include Idaho children and adolescents experiencing behavioral health
concerns and their families, particularly those residing in rural and underserved communities.
Service Area
The service area is statewide, including rural, frontier, tribal, and underserved communities across
Idaho.

be independently reviewed and scored by a review panel comprised of qualified reviewers.

Health Transformation Program priorities.
The Department reserves the right to request clarification, negotiate scope and budget, or make no
award if applications do not sufficiently meet program needs.
