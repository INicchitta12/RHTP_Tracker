Section 1: Program Description & Purpose
1.1 Background 
The Department will serve as the lead agency for administering the Rural Health Transformation Program (RHT) Grant funding award for the state of Idaho (ID). As such, the Department is committed to removing barriers to treatment, improving health outcomes, and promoting patient-centered care. The RHTP website address is:  https://healthandwelfare.idaho.gov/providers/rural-health-transformation-program-grant/about-rural-health-transformation-program-grant  
As the grant recipient, the Department manages the entire grant award and is responsible for distributing funding to other ID state agencies, contractors, and Subgrantees in alignment with the projects awarded by Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS). This distribution will be carried out in accordance with both state and federal regulation, specifically procurement rules, to facilitate the execution of RHTP work. The state’s RHTP is a critical mechanism to advance a sustainable, data-driven model of care that enhances access, quality, and outcomes.      
Idaho’s Rural Health Transformation Plan (RHTP) (Attachment A, ID RHTP Project Narrative) is a multi-year strategic plan including five (5) major initiatives designed to strengthen rural health systems, increase access to care, enhance quality outcomes, expand workforce capacity, and promote financial sustainability. These initiatives address CMS’ strategic goals for this grant to transform healthcare for residents in the state’s rural areas. 
The five initiatives are:  
Initiative 1.  Improving rural access to care through technology  
Facility technology assessments and shared infrastructure  
Telehealth expansion  
Digital health  
Cybersecurity modernization and artificial intelligence (AI) tools  
Emergency communication systems implementation and enrollment  
Health management and data analytics tools  
EHR software and upgrades  

Initiative 2.  Ensuring accessible, quality care through innovative models  
Diagnostic and care access innovations  
Health extenders  
Emergency medical service (EMS) expansion and workforce stabilization  
Understand opportunities to leverage health extenders and optimize service delivery   

Initiative 3.  Sustaining rural workforce with training, recruitment, and retention.  
Ladder payments based on priority positions and rural presence  
Healthcare career exploration and advancement programs  
Work-based learning  
Graduate medical education (GME) programs  

Initiative 4.  Implementing population specific, evidence-based projects to Make America Healthy Again  
Chronic disease prevention and treatment  
Behavioral health prevention and treatment  
Maternal and child health efforts    

Initiative 5.  Investing in rural health infrastructure and partnerships  
Healthcare facility renovations  
Pharmacy solutions  
Clinical equipment purchases  
Vehicles for patient transport and rural mobile health units  
Healthcare facility federal, state, and local safety code compliance 
Tribal rural health transformation support  

The state’s RHTP leverages partnerships with state and local agencies, tribal partners, healthcare providers, and community organizations to ensure locally responsive solutions. 

This plan will work to modernize electronic and logistical infrastructure, strengthen community-to-clinician connections, and further integrate healthcare and community-based services across settings and types of care.
1.2 Project Coordination
To ensure alignment across the Idaho Rural Health Transformation initiatives and to prevent duplication or overlap, the selected applicant must, as applicable to the scope of work, coordinate with Department staff, other Rural Health Transformation-funded entities, and key stakeholders in conducting assessment, data collection, evaluation, reporting, and development of deliverables.
1.3 Program Goals & Objectives
This solicitation specifically addresses RHTP Initiative 4: Implementing population specific, evidence-based projects to Make America Healthy Again. The goal of this funding is to reduce incidence and improve the management of chronic diseases through proven prevention, screening, and education programs tailored specifically for rural populations. Activities funded through this solicitation are encouraged to recognize and engage family caregivers when appropriate to support improved health outcomes. 
The Idaho Department of Health and Welfare (Department) seeks to award up to two (2) subgrantees to establish, administer, and oversee statewide chronic disease prevention and management initiatives and cancer screening initiatives. The successful applicant(s) may serve as pass-through organizations to local partners for implementation.


Required Activities:
Implement evidence-based chronic disease prevention and management programs in rural Idaho.
Expand access to chronic disease and cancer screening, education, and self-management supports.
Build sustainable community partnerships.
Integrate technology to overcome rural access barriers.
Improve measurable health outcomes among rural Idahoans.
Program Deliverables: Subgrantees must submit the following deliverables (at minimum):
Work plan with milestones and timelines (updated quarterly).
Evidenced-based curriculum delivery logs, including fidelity documentation.
Monthly attendance and enrollment logs for all programs implemented.
Technology utilization dashboards: logins, telehealth visits, device activation.
Devise distribution and inventory reports.
Documentation of community education events (location, reach, demographics).
Quarterly report summarizing screening activities and referrals completed.
1.4 Scope of Work
Successful applicants must work with organizations who serve rural populations to implement targeted interventions that address cancer, heart disease, and other chronic conditions. Applicants must fulfill the following approved activities:
Partnerships and Capacity Building: Coordinate and partner with the Department, hospitals, Federally Qualified Health Centers (FQHCs), Emergency Medical Service (EMS) providers, tribal entities, local public health districts and community organizations to avoid duplication, build upon existing statewide initiatives, lessons learned, and available data, and ensure rural alignment.
Participate in RHTP meetings and statewide evaluation activities
Share data with the Department for reporting, learning collaboratives and quality improvement
Align work with existing Regional Health Needs Assessments and Community Health Improvement Plans, Department priorities, and other relevant statewide planning efforts, as appropriate
Incorporate family caregivers and care partners into planning and referral processes and facilitate connections to family caregiver support resources when appropriate
Collaborate with the Department to incorporate existing statewide initiatives, implementation experience, available data, and lessons learned into project planning, implementation, and continuous quality improvement activities.
Evidence-Based Programming: Implement one or more nationally recognized evidence-based intervention, such as:
Dietitian-led nutrition program.
Structured physical activity classes.
Lifestyle behavior-change series.
Community-based chronic disease self-management.
Community-based caregiver support programs.
Establish formal referral pathways to support coordinated transitions between screening, diagnosis, evidence-based interventions, and caregiver supports.
Screening, Early Detection and Referral Expansion: Increase access to chronic disease and cancer screenings by:
Offering mobile or community-based screening events in rural settings.
Partner with rural healthcare clinics, FQHCs, and/or hospitals to expand screening capacity.
Use evidence-based risk assessments and referral pathways that align with existing statewide resources and Department-supported initiatives, when applicable.
Integrate electronic referral or follow-up systems into business procedures, electronic health record, or other patient care management processes. 

Technology Integration and Digital Health Tools: Deploy scalable, technology-based programs and tools to promote sustainable health habits and overcome rural healthcare access barriers. Programs and tools include, but are not limited to:
Telehealth platforms for coaching, education, and follow-up.
Remote patient monitoring tools (blood pressure cuffs, glucometers, scales, etc.).
Mobile health apps for self-management tracking.
Digital screening and referral tools.
Community Education, Outreach and Health Promotion: Increase rural awareness and education for rural Idahoans and their caregivers through:
Regular community workshops focused on chronic disease risk, nutrition, physical activity, cancer prevention and heart health.
Small group coaching sessions.
Community-wide campaigns delivered through rural radio, print, TV, social media, and physical locations such as schools, senior centers, libraries, businesses, etc.
Evaluation: The Subgrantee must meet at least quarterly with the Department’s Rural Health Transformation Program Data Analytics and Evaluation Contractor to complete the following tasks:  
Develop theory of change diagram(s) (e.g., logic model, line-of-sight diagram, driver diagram, or similar visualization of assumptions connecting proposed work with intended outcomes and performance indicators) 
Use data sharing mechanisms (to be developed by the Data Analytics and Evaluation contractor) to report on required key performance objectives (KPO) within timelines established by the Department which may include, but are not limited to: 
Number of sites implementing new or enhanced evidence-based prevention or treatment programs 
Number of individuals enrolling in a new or enhanced evidence-based prevention or treatment program 
Number of individuals reaching program specific retention or completion milestone 
Number of individuals who reach program specific clinical outcome (e.g., A1c, weight reduction, hypertension control) 
Other measures to be defined in consultation with the Data Analytics and Evaluation contractor after award 
Develop and implement a process evaluation and quality improvement plan that incorporates project findings, Department feedback, statewide lessons learned, and useful evaluation or process improvement measures in addition to required KPOs.
Review and provide input on draft evaluation findings, reports, and analyses as requested. 

Non-Duplication Assessment: The subgrantee must complete the Non-Duplication Assessment (Appendix H) and submit with application to ensure funds do not support an existing project or plan. 
1.5 Target Population & Service Area
The ultimate beneficiaries of this solicitation include Idahoans living in rural and frontier communities who are at risk for or living with chronic health conditions, including cardiovascular disease, stroke, cancer, and obesity.

Family caregivers and care partners may also benefit from activities supported through this solicitation. Family caregivers often play an important role in supporting prevention activities, treatment adherence, care coordination, self-management, and long-term disease management. Workforce education activities should promote awareness of caregiver engagement practices and available caregiver resources when appropriate. 

Geographic Focus and Service Area: 
Eligible service areas are rural and frontier counties within the state of Idaho, as defined by state rural health criteria and federal rural-urban classification systems, including non-metropolitan counties and rural census tracts designated under USDA Rural-Urban Continuum Codes (RUCC) or the Federal Office of Rural Health Policy (FORHP) eligibility criteria. 
See Appendices A and B for the RHTP eligibility map and a list of eligible and ineligible cities by county.
Target Audience: Individuals residing in rural areas who are at risk for, or are currently managing, chronic conditions such as cancer, heart disease, stroke, and obesity.

4.2 Project Narrative Guidance
The Project Narrative is the primary basis for evaluating the applicant’s approach and shall not exceed fifteen (15) pages. Address each area below in the order listed. Reviewers will evaluate narrative quality, specificity, and alignment with program goals. Vague or generic responses will score poorly. 
Statement of Need: What specific chronic disease does the proposed project address through prevention and appropriate disease management? Describe the specific chronic disease burden in your service area using local, regional, or state data. Identify the populations most affected and the root causes driving disparities. Explain why current resources and programs are insufficient and how this subaward will address unmet needs. Support with data.

Goals & Objectives: What measures will be tracked to demonstrate improved health outcomes; reduced disease burden; and reduced cost of care (for individuals, providers, community, state, etc.)? Use SMART (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound) criteria. For each objective, identify a baseline value, a target value, and the data source you will use to measure progress.

Approach & Methods: How will you accomplish the objectives? Describe your implementation plan in detail. Describe interventions, evidence base, and innovation. For each proposed activity, explain: the evidence data supporting the intervention, how you will adapt it to your local context, the implementation timeline, who will deliver the intervention, and how you will ensure fidelity to the model.

Target Population & Engagement: Who will be served and how will you ensure access in a rural community?  Describe the population(s) you will serve, including demographics, geographic distribution, and how you determined community priorities. Describe your approach to community engagement, including how community members will be involved in program design, implementation, and evaluation. If serving tribal communities, describe your tribal consultation process and any existing agreements.

Partnerships & Collaboration: What organizations will you work with and what roles will they play? Identify key partner organizations and describe each partner’s role, contribution, and the formal or informal agreement governing the partnership. Explain how partnerships will enhance program reach, reduce duplication, and support sustainability. Include letters of support or MOUs from each named partner as an attachment.

Evaluation Plan: How will you measure progress and outcomes? What data will be collected? Describe your plan to evaluate process outcomes (reach, dose, fidelity) and health outcomes (clinical measures, behavior change). Identify data collection methods, tools, frequency, and responsible staff. Explain how you will use evaluation data for continuous quality improvement during the funding period.

Sustainability: How will activities or outcomes continue beyond the funding period? Describe how key program activities, partnerships, or outcomes will be sustained after the subaward ends. Identify potential future funding sources, organizational commitments, and policy or systems changes that will outlast the grant.
4.3 Budget & Budget Justification Guidance
Applicants must submit a detailed, line-item budget for the full period of performance and a narrative justification for each cost category. The budget must demonstrate that all proposed costs are allowable, allocable, reasonable, and necessary per 2 CFR 200 Subpart E (§§200.400–200.476).
Personnel: List each position by title, annual salary, % FTE charged, and fringe benefit rate. Justify each position’s necessity to the project.
Fringe Benefits: Provide the fringe rate and its components (FICA, retirement, health insurance, etc.). If rates differ by employee class, break them out separately and display in a table.
Travel: Itemize trips by purpose, destination, number of travelers, and estimated costs (airfare, mileage at the federal rate, lodging, per diem). 
Equipment: Items with a per-unit cost of $2,000 or more and a useful life exceeding one year. Prior approval from the Department is required for all equipment purchases.
Supplies: Items under $2,000 per unit. Group by category (office supplies, program materials, technology) and justify quantities.
Contractual: Describe each proposed contract or subaward, including the scope, estimated cost, and procurement method. Note: subrecipients may not subaward to other entities without prior written approval from the Department.
Other Direct Costs: Includes participant incentives (limited to $25 per participant per encounter, $150 max per participant per year), printing, communications, training fees, software licenses, etc.
Administrative Costs: No more than 10% administrative costs.

5.2 Review Process
The review process consists of three stages: 
Administrative Screening: Department staff will screen applications for completeness and eligibility. Incomplete applications or those from ineligible organizations will not advance to technical review. Applicants will be notified of disqualification within 5 business days. 

Technical Review: A review panel of at least three individuals with expertise in chronic disease prevention, public health program management, and federal grants compliance will independently score each application. Reviewers will participate in a consensus scoring session to discuss and finalize scores. Panel members will sign conflict of interest disclosures. 

Final Selection: The RHTP Director or DHW Deputy Director will make final funding decisions based on panel scores, geographic coverage across the state, population reach, and programmatic priorities. The Department reserves the right to fund applications out of rank order to ensure equitable geographic distribution. 

The Department may request clarification, additional information, or interviews from applicants as part of the review process. Funding recommendations will be developed based on final evaluation scores, risk assessment results, available funding, and the Department’s goal of increasing chronic disease prevention and management initiatives, including cancer screenings, in Idaho. The Department anticipates funding one (1) to two (2) Lead Entities capable of administering and overseeing a statewide network of local implementation partners.
Final funding decisions will consider:
Application scores
Funding availability
Geographic coverage
Organizational capacity
Program priorities
Ability to achieve statewide population-level outcomes
5.3 Pre-Award Risk Assessment
All recommended applicants will undergo a pre-award risk assessment prior to subaward execution. This assessment will evaluate:
Prior experience managing federal awards of similar size and scope
Results of previous audits, including Single Audit findings (if applicable)
Ability to adhere to federal statutes, regulations, and subaward terms
Financial stability and adequacy of financial management systems
Adequacy of internal controls and policies (procurement, property, travel, etc.)
Current or pending corrective action plans

Applicants who do not pass the pre-award risk assessment may have their award rescinded or may receive the award with special conditions (e.g., more frequent reporting, limited advance payments, required technical assistance).
