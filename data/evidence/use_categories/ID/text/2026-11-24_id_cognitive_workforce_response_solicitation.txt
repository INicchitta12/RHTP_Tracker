Section 1: Program Description & Purpose
Healthcare providers serving rural communities are often responsible for identifying and addressing a broad range of health concerns, including cognitive changes that can affect an individual's health, independence, and quality of life. However, many providers, care teams, and community-based professionals have limited access to specialized education and training related to Alzheimer's disease and other dementias (ADOD), cognitive decline, dementia-capable care, caregiver engagement, and referral pathways. Expanding workforce knowledge and skills can support earlier recognition of cognitive concerns, improve care coordination, strengthen connections to available services, and improve quality of care for Idahoans living with cognitive impairment.
Opportunities for specialized education, clinical training, and workforce development related to cognitive health, dementia care, and aging remain limited in many rural areas. Partnerships among healthcare organizations, academic institutions, training programs, and community-based organizations can strengthen workforce capacity and expand access to evidence-based education and training focused on cognitive decline and dementia care.
Family caregivers, care partners, and persons are often responsible for managing the day-to-day care of individuals experiencing cognitive decline and frequently serve as essential partners in care planning, treatment adherence, and coordination of services. Improving workforce understanding of caregiver needs, available caregiver resources, and effective caregiver engagement practices can strengthen care coordination and improve outcomes for both individuals living with cognitive impairment and those who support them.
In addition to clinical knowledge, healthcare providers benefit from understanding available community resources and referral pathways that provide assistance to individuals living with Alzheimer's disease and other dementias (ADOD) and their family caregivers. Increasing awareness of services available through the Alzheimer’s Disease and Related Dementias (ADRD) Alliance, Family Caregiver Navigator Program, Community Care Program, Area Agencies on Aging, and other community partners can improve care coordination and facilitate timely access to supportive services.
The Department seeks up to two (2) organizations to establish and administer the Idaho Cognitive Health Workforce Response Network, a statewide initiative designed to strengthen workforce knowledge, improve recognition of cognitive decline, expand dementia-capable care, enhance caregiver engagement practices, and improve coordination with healthcare and community-based resources throughout Idaho.

1.3  Program Goals & Objectives
The overarching goal of this award is to strengthen Idaho's rural healthcare workforce capacity to recognize, respond to, and address cognitive health and aging-related care needs by establishing and coordinating a statewide network that improves Idaho’s response to cognitive health through the expansion of access to education, training, technical assistance, and workforce development opportunities across rural and underserved communities.
This solicitation establishes the Idaho Cognitive Health Workforce Response Network, a statewide workforce education and implementation initiative focused on strengthening Idaho's response to cognitive decline and dementia through evidence-based education, technical assistance, workforce development, and coordinated partnerships.
The Network is intended to strengthen Idaho's capacity to recognize cognitive decline earlier, improve workforce readiness, expand dementia-capable care, and improve coordination between healthcare providers, family caregivers, and community-based resources.
This solicitation aligns with Idaho's Rural Health Transformation Initiative 4: Implementing population-specific, evidence-based projects to Make America Healthy Again.
The Department anticipates making up to two (2) awards to organizations capable of administering and coordinating a statewide or multi-regional network of subgrantees. Applicants should demonstrate their ability to facilitate local implementation through partnerships, subawards, technical assistance, training, coordination, and oversight activities. The goals are tied to statewide needs assessment and strategic plans, but do not duplicate activities or supplant other funding.
Goal 1: Increase Workforce Competency in Cognitive Health and Early Recognition
Expand statewide access to education, training, and technical assistance that strengthens workforce knowledge and skills related to the early identification of Alzheimer's disease and other dementias and other forms of cognitive decline.
Goal 2: Expand Access to Cognitive Health Training and Education
Develop, coordinate, and sustain a network of organizations that provide standardized, evidence-based training opportunities related to cognitive health, dementia care, aging-related conditions, and caregiver engagement.
Goal 3: Strengthen Care Coordination and Caregiver Engagement Skills
Improve workforce capacity to effectively engage family caregivers and care partners as members of the care team, enhance care planning, and connect individuals and families to available healthcare and community-based resources.
Goal 4: Improve Integration of Cognitive Health Practices into Rural Healthcare Systems
Ensure participating organizations incorporate cognitive screening, assessment, referral, follow-up, and caregiver engagement practices into routine healthcare operations and community-based service models.
Goal 5: Expand Workforce Pipeline and Training Opportunities
Ensure development and expansion of workforce development activities, clinical training opportunities, internships, residencies, and educational partnerships that strengthen Idaho's rural cognitive health workforce.
Goal 6: Strengthen Statewide Partnerships and Long-Term Sustainability
Develop and maintain partnerships that encourage ongoing workforce education, professional development, resource sharing, recruitment, retention, and sustainability of cognitive health training efforts throughout Idaho.
RHT Initiative Alignment
This solicitation advances the following Rural Health Transformation priorities:
Strengthening rural healthcare delivery systems.
Improving care coordination across providers and community services.
Expanding access to services for aging populations.
Ensuring earlier intervention and management of chronic and cognitive health conditions.
Reducing barriers to care in rural and frontier communities.
1.4 Scope of Work
Required Activities (all sub awardees must implement):
The Department seeks up to two (2) subgrantees to develop, administer, and coordinate workforce education and training initiatives that expand access to cognitive health, dementia care, and caregiver engagement education across Idaho. Through a network of participating organizations, the selected applicant(s) will facilitate the delivery of training, workforce development activities, technical assistance, and resource coordination that strengthen the ability of rural providers, care teams, and community partners to recognize cognitive decline, assist individuals living with dementia, engage family caregivers, and connect individuals and families to appropriate services and supports.
The applicant must:
Complete the Non-Duplication Assessment (Appendix H) and submit it with the application to ensure funds do not support an existing project or plan.
Establish, administer, and coordinate the Idaho Cognitive Health Workforce Response Network through a statewide or multi-regional implementation model that improves workforce education and training related to cognitive health, aging, dementia, family caregiver engagement, and care coordination.
Develop and administer a subgrant, partnership, or implementation structure that delivers workforce education and training activities.
Provide technical assistance, coordination, and implementation to deliver approved training and workforce development activities that align with Department priorities and existing statewide cognitive health initiatives.
Ensure participating organizations deliver training opportunities for healthcare providers, healthcare students, community health workers, care coordinators, and other members of the healthcare workforce.
Promote rural clinical education, training experiences, internships, residencies, and workforce pipeline activities through partnerships with participating organizations, healthcare systems, and academic institutions.
Ensure participating organizations incorporate training on early recognition of cognitive concerns, cognitive assessment practices, referral pathways, care coordination, person-centered care, and dementia-capable practices, engaging family caregivers and care partners in assessment, care planning, and ongoing assistance.
Integrate family caregiver engagement, caregiver assessment, care partner collaboration, and awareness of caregiver resources into workforce education activities.
Promote awareness of referral pathways and services available through the Family Caregiver Navigator Program, Community Care Program, ADRD Alliance, Area Agencies on Aging, and other community-based organizations serving individuals living with Alzheimer's disease and other dementias and their caregivers.
Disseminate directly or through partners, information regarding evidence-based interventions, support services, respite resources, and educational opportunities available through community partners.
Establish and maintain partnerships with healthcare organizations, academic institutions, professional associations, workforce development entities, ADOD-related organizations, and other stakeholders necessary to achieve program goals.
Collaborate with ADRD Alliance partners and other stakeholders to identify workforce education needs and expand access to Alzheimer's disease and other dementia training opportunities throughout Idaho.
Collaborate with the Department to incorporate education regarding Idaho's Neurocognitive Crisis Hold (Idaho Code 56-2101), including the purpose of the law, implementation efforts, available data, and lessons learned to date, as appropriate for the proposed workforce education activities.
Coordinate with the Department to engage existing established Alzheimer's Disease and Related Dementias (ADRD) workgroups to ensure workforce education activities reflect existing statewide initiatives, historical knowledge, and recommendations related to cognitive health and dementia care.
Increase awareness with partner organizations’ workforce about the role family caregivers play in cognitive health, dementia care, care coordination, and maintaining independence in community settings.
Actively promote and improve recruitment, retention, professional development, and workforce capacity-building activities that strengthen expertise related to Alzheimer's disease, other dementias, cognitive decline, aging, caregiver engagement, and family-centered care.
Develop, curate, or disseminate educational materials, toolkits, clinical resources, referral guides, and implementation processes that promote adoption of dementia-capable practices.
Collect, monitor, and report information related to training activities, participation, partnerships, implementation outcomes, and workforce development efforts across participating organizations.
Submit required reports to the Department.
Participate in Department-required evaluation, monitoring, quality improvement, and continuous learning activities.
Meet at least quarterly with the Department’s Rural Health Transformation Program Data Analytics and Evaluation Contractor to complete the following tasks:   
Develop theory of change diagram(s) (e.g., logic model, line-of-sight diagram, driver diagram, or similar visualization of assumptions connecting proposed work with intended outcomes and performance indicators)  
Use data sharing mechanisms (to be developed by the Data Analytics and Evaluation contractor) to report on required key performance objectives (KPO) within timelines established by the Department which may include, but are not limited to, measures to be defined in consultation with the Data Analytics and Evaluation contractor after award.
Develop and implement a process evaluation and quality improvement plan that identifies useful evaluation or process improvement measures in addition to required KPOs.  
Review and provide input on draft evaluation findings, reports, and analyses as requested. 
1.5 Target Population & Service Area
Eligible service areas are rural and frontier counties within the state of Idaho, as defined by state rural health criteria and federal rural-urban classification systems, including non-metropolitan counties and rural census tracts designated under USDA Rural-Urban Continuum Codes (RUCC) or the Federal Office of Rural Health Policy (FORHP) eligibility criteria.
See Appendices A and B for the RHTP eligibility map and a list of eligible and ineligible cities by county.
The ultimate beneficiaries of this solicitation include adults living in communities meeting the above definition throughout Idaho who may be experiencing cognitive decline, Alzheimer's disease and other dementias (ADOD), or other cognitive concerns. This also includes individuals at increased risk for cognitive impairment due to age, chronic disease, disability, or other health-related factors.
Additional populations who may benefit from activities outlined in this solicitation include family caregivers, care partners, and other persons involved in the care of individuals experiencing cognitive decline. Because family caregivers frequently assist with care planning, daily activities, healthcare navigation, and coordination of services, workforce education efforts should promote effective caregiver engagement and awareness of available caregiver resources.
The primary recipients of training, technical assistance, workforce development activities, and educational resources developed and disseminated through this solicitation are healthcare providers, healthcare students, community health workers, care coordinators, and other members of the healthcare workforce serving rural and frontier communities. Activities should strengthen their ability to recognize cognitive concerns, engage family caregivers, connect individuals and families to available services, and ensure dementia-capable care.
Activities funded through this solicitation must benefit rural and frontier communities within Idaho where access to cognitive health expertise, specialty services, workforce development opportunities, and coordinated resources may be limited.
The Department anticipates making up to two (2) awards to organizations capable of administering and coordinating the Idaho Cognitive Health Workforce Response Network. Applicants should describe how their proposed approach will expand access to cognitive health education, workforce development, technical assistance, and training opportunities across rural Idaho through partnerships, subawards, or other implementation strategies.
Applicants should clearly define the geographic areas that will benefit from proposed activities and describe how their approach will strengthen workforce capacity and improve access to cognitive health services for Idahoans living in rural and frontier communities.
Priority Populations
Consistent with the goals of the Rural Health Transformation initiative, priority populations include:
Older adults residing in rural and frontier communities across Idaho. 
Individuals experiencing cognitive impairment, dementia, or suspected cognitive decline who face delays or barriers in identification, diagnosis, or access to services. 
Individuals with limited access to specialty care due to geographic distance, transportation challenges, workforce shortages, or other barriers to care. 
Family caregivers and care partners who assist individuals experiencing cognitive decline and may benefit from improved provider engagement and access to community resources. 
Rural healthcare providers, healthcare organizations, and community-based organizations seeking to strengthen their capacity to recognize cognitive concerns, engage family caregivers, implement dementia-capable practices, and improve care coordination. 
Applicants are encouraged to demonstrate how proposed activities will strengthen workforce capacity, improve access to cognitive health services, and address barriers to care experienced by Idahoans living in rural and frontier communities.

5.2 Review Process
The review process consists of three stages:
Stage 1 – Administrative Screening: Department staff will screen applications for completeness and eligibility. Incomplete applications or those from ineligible organizations will not advance to technical review. Applicants will be notified of disqualification within 5 business days.
Stage 2 – Technical Review: A review panel of at least three reviewers with expertise in cognitive health, dementia care, healthcare workforce development, public health program management, and federal grants compliance. Reviewers will participate in a consensus scoring session to discuss and finalize scores. Panel members will sign conflict of interest disclosures.
Stage 3 – Final Selection: The Department will make final funding decisions based on panel scores, geographic coverage across the state, population reach, and programmatic priorities. The Department reserves the right to fund applications out of rank order to ensure equitable geographic distribution.
The Department may request clarification, additional information, or interviews from applicants as part of the review process. Funding recommendations will be developed based on final evaluation scores, risk assessment results, available funding, and the Department's goal of establishing the Idaho Cognitive Health Workforce Response Network. The Department anticipates funding one (1) to two (2) Lead Entities capable of administering and overseeing a statewide network of local implementation partners.
Final funding decisions will consider:
Application scores
Funding availability
Geographic coverage
Organizational capacity
Program priorities
Ability to achieve statewide population-level outcomes

6.3 Monitoring & Oversight
The pass-through entity will monitor Subgrantee activities to ensure compliance with the subaward terms and applicable federal requirements. Monitoring activities may include:
Desk reviews of financial and programmatic reports
On-site or virtual monitoring visits (at least annually for high-risk Subgrantees)
Review of single audit reports per §200.332(d)
Technical and capacity-building assistance
Review of Subgrantee procurement documentation for compliance with §§200.318–200.327
6.4 Remedies for Noncompliance
If a Subgrantee fails to comply with the terms of the subaward or applicable federal requirements, the pass-through entity may take one or more of the following actions per §200.339:
Temporarily withhold payments pending corrective action
Disallow all or part of the cost of the noncompliant activity
Wholly or partly suspend or terminate the subaward
Initiate suspension or debarment proceedings per 2 CFR Part 180
Withhold further awards or impose additional conditions
6.5 Record Retention
Subgrantees must retain all financial records, supporting documents, statistical records, and all other records pertinent to the subaward for a period of three (3) years from the date of submission of the final expenditure report, or as otherwise required by §200.334. If any litigation, claim, or audit is started before the expiration of the retention period, records must be retained until all issues are resolved.
6.6 Procurement Standards
Subgrantees must follow the procurement standards in §§200.318–200.327, including conducting all procurement transactions in a manner providing full and open competition. Subgrantees must maintain written conflict of interest policies and document the procurement process including cost/price analysis.

Section 8: Post Award Anticipated Timeline
Following award execution, Lead Entities will be expected to complete the activities and deliverables in phases.

Phase 1: Startup and Network Development Months 1–3
Phase 2: Network Launch and Partner Engagement Months 3–6
Phase 3: Program Implementation and Workforce Education Months 6–24
Phase 4: Sustainability and Project Closeout Final 6 Months of Performance Period
Ongoing Requirements Throughout the Project Period: Lead Entities shall maintain ongoing compliance with all reporting, monitoring, and performance requirements throughout the project period.
The Department reserves the right to modify timelines, reporting schedules, or milestone expectations as necessary to ensure successful implementation of the Idaho Cognitive Health Workforce Response Network.
