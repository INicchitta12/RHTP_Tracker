Section 1: Program Description &amp; Purpose
1.1 Background 
The Department will serve as the lead agency for administering the Rural Health Transformation Program (RHT) Grant funding award for the state of Idaho (ID). As such, the Department is committed to removing barriers to treatment, improving health outcomes, and promoting patient-centered care. The RHTP website address is:  https://healthandwelfare.idaho.gov/providers/rural-health-transformation-program-grant/about-rural-health-transformation-program-grant  
As the grant recipient, the Department manages the entire grant award and is responsible for distributing funding to other ID state agencies, contractors, and subgrantees in alignment with the projects awarded by Centers for Medicare &amp; Medicaid Services (CMS). This distribution will be carried out in accordance with both state and federal regulation, specifically procurement rules, to facilitate the execution of RHTP work. The state’s RHTP is a critical mechanism to advance a sustainable, data-driven model of care that enhances access, quality, and outcomes.      
Idaho’s Rural Health Transformation Plan (RHTP) (Attachment A, ID RHTP Project Narrative) is a multi-year strategic plan including five (5) major initiatives designed to strengthen rural health systems, increase access to care, enhance quality outcomes, expand workforce capacity, and promote financial sustainability. These initiatives address CMS’ strategic goals for this grant to transform healthcare for residents in the state’s rural areas. 
The five initiatives are: 
Initiative 1.  Improving rural access to care through technology  
Facility technology assessments and shared infrastructure  
Telehealth expansion  
Digital health  
Cybersecurity modernization and artificial intelligence (AI) tools  
Emergency communication systems implementation and enrollment  
Health management and data analytics tools  
EHR software and upgrades  

Initiative 2.  Ensuring accessible, quality care through innovative models  
Diagnostic and care access innovations  
Health extenders  
Emergency medical service (EMS) expansion and workforce stabilization  
Understand opportunities to leverage health extenders and optimize service delivery   

Initiative 3.  Sustaining rural workforce with training, recruitment, and retention.  
Ladder payments based on priority positions and rural presence  
Healthcare career exploration and advancement programs  
Work-based learning  
Graduate medical education (GME) programs  

Initiative 4.  Implementing population specific, evidence-based projects to Make America Healthy Again  
Chronic disease prevention and treatment  
Behavioral health prevention and treatment  
Maternal and child health efforts    

Initiative 5.  Investing in rural health infrastructure and partnerships  
Healthcare facility renovations  
Pharmacy solutions  
Clinical equipment purchases  
Vehicles for patient transport and rural mobile health units  
Healthcare facility federal, state, and local safety code compliance 
Tribal rural health transformation support  

The state’s RHTP leverages partnerships with state and local agencies, tribal partners, healthcare providers, and community organizations to ensure locally responsive solutions. This program will work to modernize electronic and logistical infrastructure, strengthen community to clinician connections, and further integrate healthcare and community-based services across 
settings and types of care.

1.2 Project Coordination
To ensure alignment across the Idaho Rural Health Transformation Program and to prevent duplication or overlap, the selected applicants must, as applicable to the scope of work, coordinate with Department staff, other Rural Health Transformation Program-funded entities, and key stakeholders in conducting assessment, data collection, evaluation, reporting, and development of deliverables. 

1.3  Program Goals &amp; Objectives
The Department seeks to expand healthcare capacity in rural and frontier Idaho communities by addressing identified facility infrastructure gaps that impede delivery of healthcare to existing and expanded populations. Through this funding opportunity, investments will be made in equipment, technology, connectivity, training, mobile health options, and targeted facility renovations. This opportunity will support shared service and technology models that help smaller facilities reduce IT costs, strengthen interoperability, and improve regional coordination. 
The expected impact of this funding includes:
Modernization of healthcare facilities to allow for implementation of innovative care models.
Decreased travel time for rural residents to receive higher level services closer to home.
Increased revenue for rural provider entities now able to offer expanded access to existing services or new services and supports.
Decreased transportation barriers to improve access to healthcare.
Reduced transfers of care (particularly to hospitals for preventable admissions), improved continuity of care, and increased access to preventive and specialty services for rural residents. 
Increased early detection of disease, improved medication adherence, and reduced travel barriers for rural residents preventing and managing chronic conditions.
Enhanced telehealth capacity in rural and frontier communities through hospitals, clinics, and EMS agencies. 
Improved chronic disease self-management, higher patient engagement, reduced hospital readmissions, and increased appropriate follow up care. 
Reduced risk of critical safety incidents through improvements to physical healthcare structures and increased compliance with local, state, and federal safety codes.
 Enhanced role of pharmacies and pharmacists in rural areas.
Strengthened cybersecurity, reduced risk of data breaches, and improved analytical capacity for patient care. 
Together, these efforts will modernize rural healthcare infrastructure, expand service delivery capacity, and strengthen continuity of care across Idaho’s most hard-to-reach communities. This supports RHTP Initiatives: 
1: Improving rural access to care through technology 
3: Sustaining rural workforce with training, recruitment, and retention
5: Investing in rural health infrastructure and partnerships

1.5 Target Population &amp; Service Area
Primary Target Population
The primary target population for this subgrant opportunity is residents of rural and frontier Idaho communities who face geographic, financial, or structural barriers to accessing timely, high-quality health services. Rural and frontier communities often experience challenges related to long travel distances, limited health care workforce capacity, and economic constraints that can affect access to care. Populations of particular focus include:
Individuals living in rural and frontier counties, including those with no local hospital, or limited access to occupational care, primary care, or pharmacy services. 
Individuals in federally designated Health Professional Shortage Areas or Medically Underserved Areas
Low-income individuals and families, including those on Medicaid or without insurance
Individuals with chronic health conditions who require regular access to care that is not locally available
Older adults and individuals with physical disabilities for whom distance and transportation create significant barriers
Individuals experiencing behavioral health conditions, including substance use disorders, in communities with limited or no local behavioral health services
American Indian and Alaska Native populations, including members of federally recognized tribes in rural Idaho
Maternal and child health populations in communities with limited obstetric or pediatric services
Geographic Service Area
Eligible service areas are rural and frontier counties within the state of Idaho, as defined by state rural health criteria and federal rural-urban classification systems, including non-metropolitan counties and rural census tracts designated under USDA Rural-Urban Continuum Codes (RUCC) or the Federal Office of Rural Health Policy (FORHP) eligibility criteria. 
Idaho's rural and frontier communities face unique structural challenges not present in urban settings, including long travel distances to tertiary care, limited broadband and telecommunications infrastructure, a high concentration of Critical Access Hospitals operating on thin margins, and persistent shortages of primary care and specialty providers. This solicitation is designed specifically to address those conditions.
Applicants must:
Clearly define the geographic area to be served, including specific counties, zip codes, or census tracts
Provide local data or a qualifying assessment of need (per Section 1.4) documenting healthcare access gaps in the defined service area
Demonstrate that the proposed activities are responsive to the specific rural or frontier conditions of the service area, not simply transportable from an urban model
Multi-county and regional applications are encouraged where a coordinated approach reduces duplication, improves coverage, or better reflects how rural residents access care across county lines.
See Appendices A and B for the RHTP eligibility map and a list of eligible and ineligible cities by county.

Initial applications (Stage 1) must identify the applicant’s need in detail (see Project Narrative), provide a reasonable workplan to ensure funding can be spent within defined timeframes of the subgrant, and a basic budget that sufficiently identifies the funding need and scope of the project. After Stage 1 applications are evaluated, the Department will issue conditional awards based on application documentation with a requirement to provide a revised budget and workplan within 45 days of the subgrant award (Stage 2). 5.2 Review Process
Applications will be screened for completeness and eligibility upon receipt of the application. Complete, eligible applications will be independently scored by a review panel of no fewer than three qualified reviewers. Final funding decisions will be made by the RHTP Program Director based on review panel scores, geographic distribution, and programmatic priorities.

In the event of tied scores, the Department will prioritize applications from Critical Access Hospitals, facilities serving the highest proportion of Medicaid and uninsured patients, or applicants in counties with the fewest existing healthcare resources.

5.3 Pre-Award Risk Assessment
All recommended applicants will undergo a pre-award risk assessment prior to subaward execution. This assessment will evaluate:
Prior experience managing federal awards of similar size and scope.
Results of the most recent Single Audit (if applicable), including the type of opinion, material 
weaknesses, significant deficiencies, and findings specific to federal programs. This requirement applies to any organization that has spent more than $1,000,000 from a federal source in the prior year.  
Financial stability indicators, including current ratio, unrestricted net assets, and recent year-over-year revenue trends. 
Adequacy of the organization’s financial management system, including ability to track costs by funding source per §200.302. 
Written policies for procurement (§§200.318–327), travel, property management, and time and-effort reporting. 
History of performance on prior Department subawards or other state/federal awards. 
New organizations or those with identified risk factors will be designated as “high risk” and subject to enhanced monitoring conditions per §200.208.

The Department reserves the right to require additional documentation, impose specific award conditions, or decline to make an award to an applicant based on the results of the pre-award risk assessment, notwithstanding the applicant's evaluation score.

6.3 Monitoring &amp; Oversight
The Department will implement a risk-based monitoring strategy consistent with §200.332(d): 
Standard-risk Subrecipients: Desk review of all financial and programmatic reports; one scheduled monitoring visit (virtual or on-site) per year; annual review of Single Audit report. 

High-risk Subrecipients: Monthly desk reviews; quarterly monitoring calls; one on-site monitoring visit per year; limited to cost-reimbursement payment method (no advance payments); required to submit supporting documentation with each reimbursement request. 
The subrecipient will receive a monitoring notification letter at least 30 days before a scheduled 
visit. Monitoring visit reports will be issued within 30 days. The subrecipient must respond to any 
findings within 30 days of receipt. The Department will issue a management decision on all findings 
within 6 months per §200.332(d)(3).

6.4 Remedies for Noncompliance
If a subrecipient fails to comply with the terms of the subaward or applicable federal requirements, the pass-through entity may take one or more of the following actions per §200.339:
Temporarily withhold payments pending corrective action
Disallow all or part of the cost of the noncompliant activity
Wholly or partly suspend or terminate the subaward
Initiate suspension or debarment proceedings per 2 CFR Part 180
Withhold further awards or impose additional conditions

Subrecipients must follow the procurement standards in §§200.318–200.327, including conducting all procurement transactions in a manner providing full and open competition.
