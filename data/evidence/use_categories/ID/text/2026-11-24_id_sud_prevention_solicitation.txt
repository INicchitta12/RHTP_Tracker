Section 1: Program Description & Purpose
The purpose of this solicitation is to support eligible entities with the implementation of evidence-based behavioral health and substance misuse prevention-focused parent, family, and home-visit programs in previously unserved rural communities – with the goal of strengthening the prevention capacity and expanding access to family-focused prevention programming across Idaho’s rural counties. 
Subgrant recipients will receive funding to participate in training on recognized evidence-based programs (EBPs), and to integrate and implement these programs into their service delivery systems and the existing community infrastructure in a sustainable manner.
Rural communities across Idaho face unique substance misuse challenges shaped by local risk and protective factors, compounded by limited access to prevention resources. Systematic reviews and outcome assessments consistently show that parent, family, and home-visit programs are effective at preventing youth substance misuse, reducing parental substance misuse, and decreasing the likelihood of the development of substance use disorders.
This RHTP solicitation is grounded in the evidence that parent and family engagement and education are among the most effective upstream prevention strategies available. Expanding these programs to previously unserved rural communities represent a strategic, long-term investment in Idaho's behavioral health and substance misuse prevention efforts.
All activities must comply with the Idaho Parental Rights Act, Idaho Code §§ 32-1010-1015.
1.1 Background 
The Department will serve as the lead agency for administering the Rural Health Transformation Program (RHT) Grant funding award for the state of Idaho (ID). As such, the Department is committed to removing barriers to treatment, improving health outcomes, and promoting patient-centered care. The RHTP website address is:  https://healthandwelfare.idaho.gov/providers/rural-health-transformation-program-grant/about-rural-health-transformation-program-grant  
As the grant recipient, the Department manages the entire grant award and is responsible for distributing funding to other ID state agencies, contractors, and Subgrantees in alignment with the projects awarded by Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS). This distribution will be carried out in accordance with both state and federal regulation, specifically procurement rules, to facilitate the execution of RHTP work. The state’s RHTP is a critical mechanism to advance a sustainable, data-driven model of care that enhances access, quality, and outcomes.      
Idaho’s Rural Health Transformation Plan (RHTP) (Attachment A, ID RHTP Project Narrative) is a multi-year strategic plan including five (5) major initiatives designed to strengthen rural health systems, increase access to care, enhance quality outcomes, expand workforce capacity, and promote financial sustainability. These initiatives address CMS’ strategic goals for this grant to transform healthcare for residents in the state’s rural areas.

To ensure alignment across the Idaho Rural Health Transformation initiatives and to prevent duplication or overlap, the selected applicant must, as applicable to the scope of work, coordinate with Department staff, other Rural Health Transformation-funded entities, and key stakeholders in conducting assessment, data collection, evaluation, reporting, and development of deliverables.
1.3 Program Goals & Objectives 
This specific solicitation supports the behavioral health prevention and treatment strategy within the RHTP initiative #4– Implementing population specific, evidence-based projects to Make America Health Again.
This funding opportunity is guided by two overarching goals and their associated objectives. The following goals and objectives will guide the development of each subgrant recipient’s project proposal and action plan. All selected recipients are expected to address each objective within their solicitation application and during the grant award period. Goal 1: Increase Access to Evidence-based Substance Misuse Prevention-focused Parent, Family, or Home-visit Programs in Rural Communities 
Reduce geographic disparities in prevention programming access by prioritizing expansion into rural communities that currently lack evidence-based parent, family, or home-visit prevention programming, ensuring residents have meaningful access to high-quality behavioral health and substance misuse prevention services. All activities must comply with the Idaho Parental Rights Act, Idaho Code §§ 32-1010-1015. 
Goal 2: Expand Organizational and Community Capacity for the Implementation of Evidence-based Substance Misuse Prevention-focused Parent, Family, and Home-visit Programs 
Support eligible public and nonprofit entities with the expansion of existing, or implementation of new, evidenced based behavioral health and substance misuse programming. Any programming selected must be prevention-focused and aimed at building organizational competency and program delivery capacity in rural Idaho communities. Requirements of subgrant recipients include:
Selection of at least one evidence-based substance misuse prevention-focused parent, family, or home-visit program for implementation in a previously unserved rural community.
Completion of required training for the selected programs by staff directly involved or overseeing implementation of the prevention programming. Training must be obtained through recognized program developers or their authorized training entities.
Collaboration with community stakeholders to build buy-in and integrate programming into existing infrastructure for long-term sustainability.
1.4 Scope of Work
The applicant must complete the Non-Duplication Assessment (Appendix H) and submit with application to ensure funds do not support an existing project or plan. 
The selected applicant shall implement evidence-based parent, family, and/or home-visit prevention programming that expands access to behavioral health and substance misuse prevention services in rural Idaho communities. The selected applicant shall complete the following required activities throughout the grant award period.
Required Activities
Evidence-Based Program Implementation
Select and implement at least one evidence-based parent, family, or home-visit program focused on substance misuse prevention and behavioral health promotion.
Implement programming in one or more rural communities identified in the approved application that currently have limited or no access to the selected evidence-based program.
Deliver the minimum number of program cohorts, classes, sessions, or home visits identified in the application.
Recruit, enroll, and serve participants from the identified target population(s).
Implement programming with fidelity to the evidence-based program model while documenting any approved adaptations consistent with developer guidance.
Applicants shall describe how the proposed program will be delivered, including the staffing model required for successful implementation. Prevention programming may include models requiring instruction or facilitation by licensed or credentialed clinicians as well as models that may be delivered by trained non-clinical staff, paraprofessionals, educators, or other qualified personnel, as appropriate for the selected evidence-based program.
Service Delivery
Applicants shall describe the proposed service delivery approach, including how services will be made accessible to rural communities. Delivery models may include: 
In-person programming 
Telehealth or hybrid service delivery 
Mobile or community-based programming 
Regional hub-and-spoke models that leverage centralized expertise while supporting local implementation across multiple communities 
Other innovative approaches that improve access to evidence-based prevention services.
Workforce Development and Training
Ensure staff directly involved in or responsible for overseeing program implementation complete all required training for the selected evidence-based program(s).
Obtain training through the recognized program developer or an authorized training provider.
Maintain documentation of completed trainings and any required certifications or credentials.
Community Collaboration
Collaborate with community partners throughout the grant period to support participant recruitment, referral pathways, and program implementation.
Build community awareness and stakeholder support for prevention programming.
Integrate programming into existing community systems and infrastructure to strengthen long-term sustainability.
Coordinate with local behavioral health providers, healthcare organizations, schools, community-based organizations, tribal organizations, and other relevant partners, as applicable.
Data Collection and Evaluation
Collect participant-level, process, and outcome data necessary to measure program implementation, participant outcomes, and program fidelity.
Submit required quarterly and annual progress reports
Report program implementation activities, participant enrollment and completion, program delivery, workforce development activities, and progress toward performance measures.
Participate in state- and provider-level evaluation activities and provide additional documentation upon request.
Program Administration and Sustainability
All proposals must represent new or enhanced services that are not duplicative of existing reimbursable services.
Applicants must submit a sustainability plan describing how services or program capacity will be maintained beyond the grant period. Sustainability plans may not include an expectation Medicaid will reimburse for services that are not currently coverable by Mediciad.  
For activities that can be completed or established through one-time funding, applicants must explain how the investment will result in lasting benefits, such as trained personnel, organizational capacity, reusable materials, established infrastructure, or continued access to prevention programming. 
1.5 Target Population & Service Area
Target population: Youth, adults, and families in rural Idaho communities.
Geographic Service Area(s):Eligible service areas include designated rural and frontier counties within the state, as defined by state rural health criteria and/or federal rural-urban classification systems (e.g., non-metropolitan counties or rural census tracts). Applicants must clearly define the geographic area to be served and provide justification for need based on local data, community health assessments, or documented service gaps.
