Section 1: Program Description & Purpose
1.1 Background  
The Department will serve as the lead agency for administering the Rural Health Transformation Program (RHTP) Grant funding award for the state of Idaho (ID). As such, the Department is committed to removing barriers to treatment, improving health outcomes, and promoting patient-centered care. The RHTP website address is:  https://healthandwelfare.idaho.gov/providers/rural-health-transformation-program-grant/about-rural-health-transformation-program-grant   
As the grant recipient, the Department manages the entire grant award and is responsible for distributing funding to other ID state agencies, contractors, and subrecipients in alignment with the projects awarded by Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS). This distribution will be carried out in accordance with both state and federal regulation, specifically procurement rules, to facilitate the execution of RHTP work. The state’s RHTP is a critical mechanism to advance a sustainable, data-driven model of care that enhances access, quality, and outcomes.       

Idaho’s RHTP (Attachment A, ID RHTP Project Narrative) is a multi-year strategic plan including five (5) major initiatives designed to strengthen rural health systems, increase access to care, enhance quality outcomes, expand workforce capacity, and promote financial sustainability. These initiatives address CMS’ strategic goals for this grant to transform healthcare for residents in the state’s rural areas.  

All activities must comply with the Idaho Parental Rights Act, Idaho Code §§ 32-1010-1015

The five initiatives are:   

Initiative 1.  Improving rural access to care through technology   
Facility technology assessments and shared infrastructure   
Telehealth expansion   
Digital health   
Cybersecurity modernization and artificial intelligence (AI) tools   
Emergency communication systems implementation and enrollment   
Health management and data analytics tools   
EHR software and upgrades   

Initiative 2.  Ensuring accessible, quality care through innovative models   
Diagnostic and care access innovations   
Health extenders   
Emergency medical service (EMS) expansion and workforce stabilization   
Understand opportunities to leverage health extenders and optimize service delivery    

Initiative 3.  Sustaining rural workforce with training, recruitment, and retention.   
Ladder payments based on priority positions and rural presence   
Healthcare career exploration and advancement programs   
Work-based learning   
Graduate medical education (GME) programs   


Initiative 4.  Implementing population specific, evidence-based projects to Make America Healthy Again   
Chronic disease prevention and treatment   
Behavioral health prevention and treatment   
Maternal and child health efforts     

Initiative 5.  Investing in rural health infrastructure and partnerships   
Healthcare facility renovations   
Pharmacy solutions   
Clinical equipment purchases   
Vehicles for patient transport and rural mobile health units   
Healthcare facility federal, state, and local safety code compliance  
Tribal rural health transformation support   
 
The state’s RHTP leverages partnerships with state and local agencies, tribal partners, healthcare providers, and community organizations to ensure locally responsive solutions.  
This program will work to modernize electronic and logistical infrastructure, strengthen community-to-clinician connections, and further integrate healthcare and community-based services across settings and types of care. 

This solicitation supports Initiative 4: Implementing Population Specific, Evidence-Based Projects to Make America Healthy Again, specifically behavioral health prevention and treatment and maternal and child health efforts.

1.2 Project Coordination 
To ensure alignment across the Idaho Rural Health Transformation Program and to prevent duplication or overlap, the selected applicant must, as applicable to the scope of work, coordinate with Department staff, other Rural Health Transformation Program-funded entities, and key stakeholders in conducting assessment, data collection, evaluation, reporting, and development of deliverables. 

The selected applicant must incorporate and align with existing and related work identified by the Department and must not duplicate efforts. Deliverables must demonstrate alignment and meet Department-defined quality and completeness standards. 
1.3 Program Goals & Objectives
The Department seeks to award funding to applicants that propose innovative, evidence-based behavioral health prevention initiatives that expand access to prevention services in rural and underserved communities throughout Idaho. Proposed projects should strengthen the behavioral health continuum of care by promoting protective factors, reducing behavioral health risk factors, supporting early identification and intervention, and improving overall community well-being. All activities must comply with the Idaho Parental Rights Act, Idaho Code §§ 32-1010-1015.
Projects may serve individuals of any age, including children, youth, families, adults, older adults, or other priority populations identified through local needs assessments and community data.
Proposed projects should support one or more of the following objectives:
Expand access to evidence-based behavioral health prevention services in rural and underserved communities. 
Improve early identification of behavioral health needs and connect individuals with appropriate prevention and support services. 
Strengthening individual, family, and community protective factors that promote resilience and behavioral health. 
Increase the availability and accessibility of prevention programming through innovative service delivery models, including community-based, school-based, telehealth, mobile, or regional hub-and-spoke approaches. 
Build sustainable local capacity through workforce development, community partnerships, and implementation of prevention programs that provide lasting benefit beyond the grant period. 
This solicitation supports the following Rural Health Transformation Program priorities:
Assisting rural communities in strengthening and right sizing their healthcare delivery systems through accessible prevention services. 
Promoting evidence-based, measurable interventions that improve behavioral health outcomes and overall community health.
1.4 Scope of Work
The applicant must complete the Non-Duplication Assessment (Appendix H) and submit with application to ensure funds do not support an existing project or plan. 

Behavioral Health Prevention Programming
Implement new or enhanced evidence-based behavioral health prevention programming designed to improve behavioral health outcomes and strengthen protective factors within the target population.
Programming may include universal, selective, or indicated prevention approaches, early identification and intervention activities, caregiver and family education, community-based prevention initiatives, or other evidence-based prevention strategies appropriate for the proposed population.
Proposed programming may include interventions that require instruction or facilitation by licensed or credentialed clinicians, as well as interventions that may be delivered by trained non-clinical professionals, paraprofessionals, educators, or other qualified personnel, as appropriate for the selected evidence-based model.

Service Delivery
Applicants shall describe how services will be delivered to maximize access for rural communities.
Service delivery may include in-person, telehealth, hybrid, mobile, school-based, community-based, or other innovative approaches that improve accessibility. 
Applicants may propose regional service delivery models, including hub and spoke approaches that leverage centralized expertise while supporting local implementation across multiple communities.




Workforce Development
Funds may support workforce development activities necessary to implement prevention programming including training, certification, train the trainer initiatives, supervision, consultation and technical assistance.

Sustainability
All proposals must represent new or enhanced services that are not duplicative of existing reimbursable services.  Applicants must submit a sustainability plan describing how services or program capacity will be maintained beyond the grant period.  Sustainability plans may not include an expectation Medicaid will reimburse for services that are not currently coverable by Medicaid.  
For activities that can be completed or established through one-time funding, applicants must explain how the investment will result in lasting benefits, such as trained personnel, organizational capacity, reusable materials, established infrastructure, or continued access to prevention programming. 

Allowable use of funds
Funds may be used for activities necessary to establish, expand, and sustain prevention programming, including but not limited to personnel, training, implementation support, telehealth infrastructure, outreach, educational materials, program supplies, technology, and other costs directly related to successful implementation. 
1.5 Target Population & Service Area
Individuals of all ages residing in rural and underserved areas of Idaho who may benefit from behavioral health prevention services. Proposed projects may target specific populations based on demonstrated community need, including but not limited to children, youth, families, older adults, individuals at increased risk for behavioral health conditions, or other priority populations identified through local data and community assessment.

2.2 Minimum Qualifications

Minimum qualifications include: 
Unique Entity Identifier (UEI): Have a valid UEI, the federal identification number assigned through SAM.gov.
SAM.gov Registration: Have an active SAM.gov registration, if required for receipt of the award. Applicants should verify their registration status before applying.
Federal Eligibility: Be eligible to receive federal funds and have no active exclusions or debarments that prohibit the organization from receiving a federally funded award.
Single Audit: If the organization is subject to federal Single Audit requirements (spent $1 million in federal funds in the last year), have completed the required audit for the applicable fiscal year.
Relevant Experience: Demonstrate experience, expertise, or organizational capability relevant to the public health activities proposed in the application.
Federal Grant Compliance: Demonstrate the organizational capacity to manage federal funds and comply with applicable requirements of 2 CFR Part 200 (Uniform Guidance). This includes appropriate financial management, recordkeeping, internal controls, procurement, and oversight practices, as applicable to the award.
RHTP Alignment: Demonstrate that the proposed use of funds supports the goals and allowable activities of the Idaho Rural Health Transformation Program (RHTP) and applicable Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) requirements and guidance.
Applicants who are unfamiliar with federal grant requirements are encouraged to review the application guidance and available technical assistance resources. Lack of prior experience receiving federal funds does not necessarily disqualify an applicant; however, selected applicants must be able to meet all applicable federal and program requirements associated with the award. Applicants that do not meet all minimum qualifications may be deemed ineligible and may not be considered for funding.
2.3 Exclusions
The following entities are not eligible to apply for funding under this subgrant solicitation: 
Organizations that are debarred, suspended, or otherwise excluded from participation in federal programs, as verified through the SAM.gov 
Applicants that do not maintain active registration in SAM.gov 
Applicants with unresolved audit findings, including outstanding single audit findings or material weaknesses that have not been adequately addressed to the satisfaction of the awarding agency 
Applicants that do not have the financial management systems and internal controls necessary to manage federal funds in accordance with applicable requirements 
Individuals applying in a personal capacity 
The awarding agency reserves the right to verify eligibility and disqualify applicants that do not meet federal and state compliance requirements.

5.2 Review Process
The review process consists of three stages: 
Stage 1 – Administrative Screening: Department staff will screen applications for completeness and eligibility. Incomplete applications or those from ineligible applicants will not advance to technical review. Applicants will be notified of disqualification within 5 business days. 
Stage 2 – Technical Review: A review panel of at least three individuals with expertise in chronic disease prevention, public health program management, and federal grants compliance will independently score each application. Reviewers will participate in a consensus scoring session to discuss and finalize scores. Panel members will sign conflict of interest disclosures. 
Stage 3 – Final Selection: The Department will make final funding decisions based on panel scores, geographic coverage across the state, population reach, and programmatic priorities. The Department reserves the right to fund applications out of rank order to ensure equitable geographic distribution. 
The Department may request clarification, additional information, or interviews from applicants as part of the review process. Funding recommendations will be developed based on final evaluation scores, risk assessment results, available funding, and the Department’s goal of establishing an effective Statewide Chronic Disease Education Network. The Department anticipates funding one (1) Lead Entities capable of administering and overseeing a statewide network of local partners. 
Final funding decisions will consider: 
Application scores 
Funding availability 
Geographic coverage 
Organizational capacity 
Program priorities 
Ability to achieve statewide population-level outcomes 
5.3 Pre-Award Risk Assessment
All recommended applicants will undergo a pre-award risk assessment prior to subaward execution. This assessment will evaluate:
Prior experience managing federal awards of similar size and complexity 
Results of the most recent Single Audit (if applicable), including the type of opinion, material weaknesses, significant deficiencies, and findings specific to federal programs 
Financial stability indicators, including current ratio, unrestricted net assets, and recent year-over-year revenue trends 
Adequacy of the organization’s financial management system, including ability to track costs by funding source per §200.302 
Written policies for procurement (§§200.318–327), travel, property management, and time-and-effort reporting 
History of performance on prior Department subawards or other state/federal awards 
New organizations or those with identified risk factors will be designated as “high risk” and subject to enhanced monitoring conditions per §200.208
