Section 1: Program Description & Purpose
Chronic diseases remain among the leading causes of illness, disability, healthcare utilization, and mortality in Idaho. Many chronic health conditions require ongoing prevention, early identification, treatment, and long-term management, including cardiovascular disease, stroke, diabetes, cancer, Alzheimer's disease and other dementias, obesity and metabolic conditions, neurodevelopmental disorders, mental health conditions, and immune-related conditions. Rural healthcare providers frequently serve as the primary point of care for individuals living with or at risk for these conditions but may have limited access to current evidence-based education, specialty expertise, and continuing professional development opportunities, particularly in rural and frontier communities.
Expanding access to high-quality education, technical assistance, and peer-to-peer learning through a Statewide Chronic Disease Education Network will strengthen clinical practice, promote evidence-based prevention and treatment, improve care coordination, and support earlier identification and long-term management of chronic health conditions across Idaho.
All activities must comply with the Idaho Parental Rights Act, Idaho Code §§ 32-1010-1015
1.1 Background 
The Department will serve as the lead agency for administering the Rural Health Transformation Program (RHT) Grant funding award for the state of Idaho (ID). As such, the Department is committed to removing barriers to treatment, improving health outcomes, and promoting patient-centered care. The RHTP website address is:  https://healthandwelfare.idaho.gov/providers/rural-health-transformation-program-grant/about-rural-health-transformation-program-grant  
As the grant recipient, the Department manages the entire grant award and is responsible for distributing funding to other ID state agencies, contractors, and Subgrantees in alignment with the projects awarded by Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS). This distribution will be carried out in accordance with both state and federal regulation, specifically procurement rules, to facilitate the execution of RHTP work. The state’s RHTP is a critical mechanism to advance a sustainable, data-driven model of care that enhances access, quality, and outcomes.      
Idaho’s Rural Health Transformation Plan (RHTP) (Attachment A, ID RHTP Project Narrative) is a multi-year strategic plan including five (5) major initiatives designed to strengthen rural health systems, increase access to care, enhance quality outcomes, expand workforce capacity, and promote financial sustainability. These initiatives address CMS’ strategic goals for this grant to transform healthcare for residents in the state’s rural areas. 
The five initiatives are:  
Initiative 1.  Improving rural access to care through technology  
Facility technology assessments and shared infrastructure  
Telehealth expansion  
Digital health  
Cybersecurity modernization and artificial intelligence (AI) tools  
Emergency communication systems implementation and enrollment  
Health management and data analytics tools  
EHR software and upgrades  
Initiative 2.  Ensuring accessible, quality care through innovative models  
Diagnostic and care access innovations  
Health extenders  
Emergency medical service (EMS) expansion and workforce stabilization  
Understand opportunities to leverage health extenders and optimize service delivery   
Initiative 3.  Sustaining rural workforce with training, recruitment, and retention.  
Ladder payments based on priority positions and rural presence  
Healthcare career exploration and advancement programs  
Work-based learning  
Graduate medical education (GME) programs  
Initiative 4.  Implementing population specific, evidence-based projects to Make America Healthy Again  
Chronic disease prevention and treatment  
Behavioral health prevention and treatment  
Maternal and child health efforts    
Initiative 5.  Investing in rural health infrastructure and partnerships  
Healthcare facility renovations  
Pharmacy solutions  
Clinical equipment purchases  
Vehicles for patient transport and rural mobile health units  
Healthcare facility federal, state, and local safety code compliance 
Tribal rural health transformation support  

The state’s RHTP leverages partnerships with state and local agencies, tribal partners, healthcare providers, and community organizations to ensure locally responsive solutions. 

This plan will work to modernize electronic and logistical infrastructure, strengthen community-to-clinician connections, and further integrate healthcare and community-based services across settings and types of care.
1.2 Project Coordination
To ensure alignment across the Idaho Rural Health Transformation initiatives and to prevent duplication or overlap, the selected applicant must, as applicable to the scope of work, coordinate with Department staff, other Rural Health Transformation-funded entities, and key stakeholders in conducting assessment, data collection, evaluation, reporting, and development of deliverables.
1.3  Program Goals & Objectives
This solicitation specifically addresses RHTP Initiative 4: Implementing population specific, evidence-based projects to Make America Healthy Again, by establishing a Statewide Chronic Disease Education Network. The Department anticipates making one (1) award to an organization capable of administering and coordinating a statewide network that expands access to evidence-based education, training, technical assistance, and professional learning opportunities related to chronic disease prevention and management. The selected applicant (subgrantee) may implement the deliverables through partnerships, subawards, subject matter experts, learning collaboratives, continuing education opportunities, and other strategies that strengthen Idaho's rural healthcare workforces’ education and understanding of chronic disease reduction and management measures. All activities must comply with the Idaho Parental Rights Act, Idaho Code §§ 32-1010-1015.
The selected subgrantee must administer activities that align with each of the following goals:
Goal 1
Expand access to evidence-based education related to chronic disease prevention and management.
Goal 2
Improve Idaho’s allied and clinical healthcare workforce knowledge and confidence in addressing priority chronic conditions.
Goal 3
Increase adoption of evidence-based prevention, screening, referral, and treatment practices.
Goal 4
Strengthen connections between rural providers, specialists, public health programs, community resources, and caregiver support systems.
Goal 5
Improve provider understanding of family caregiver engagement and the role caregivers play in chronic disease management.
Goal 6
Establish a sustainable Statewide Chronic Disease Education Network.

1.5 Target Population & Service Area
The primary recipients of activities supported through this solicitation are allied and clinical healthcare professionals and members of the healthcare workforce serving rural and frontier communities throughout Idaho. This includes physicians, advanced practice providers, nurses, pharmacists, behavioral health professionals, social workers, community health workers, care coordinators, public health professionals, healthcare students, trainees, and other professionals involved in the prevention, identification, treatment, management, and coordination of care for individuals living with chronic health conditions.
The ultimate beneficiaries of this solicitation include Idahoans living in rural and frontier communities who are at risk for or are living with chronic health conditions, including cardiovascular disease, stroke, diabetes, cancer, Alzheimer's disease and other dementias, obesity and metabolic conditions, neurodevelopmental disorders, mental health conditions, and immune or allergic conditions.
Family caregivers and care partners are important participants in chronic disease management and may also benefit from activities supported through this solicitation. Family caregivers often play an important role in supporting prevention activities, treatment adherence, care coordination, self-management, and long-term disease management. Workforce education activities should promote awareness of caregiver engagement practices and available caregiver resources when appropriate.
Activities funded through this solicitation must benefit rural and frontier communities across Idaho where access to specialty expertise, continuing education opportunities, workforce development resources, and evidence-based clinical guidance may be limited.
The Department anticipates making one (1) award to an applicant capable of administering and coordinating a Statewide Chronic Disease Education Network. Applicants should describe how their proposed model will ensure broad geographic reach and equitable access to educational opportunities across Idaho through partnerships, subawards, learning collaboratives, technical assistance, virtual learning platforms, or other implementation strategies.
Applicants should clearly describe how their proposed structure will strengthen workforce knowledge, expand access to evidence-based education, and improve the prevention, identification, treatment, and management of chronic health conditions across rural Idaho.
Priority Populations
Consistent with the goals of the Rural Health Transformation initiative, priority populations include:
Healthcare professionals serving rural and frontier communities.
Healthcare organizations seeking to strengthen workforce knowledge and implementation of evidence-based chronic disease prevention and treatment practices in rural Idaho.
Individuals living in rural Idaho with or at increased risk for cardiovascular disease, stroke, diabetes, cancer, Alzheimer's disease and other dementias, obesity and metabolic conditions, neurodevelopmental disorders, mental health conditions, and immune or allergic conditions.
Individuals in rural Idaho experiencing barriers to specialty care, prevention services, or evidence-based treatment due to geographic distance, workforce shortages, transportation challenges, or other barriers to care.
Family caregivers and care partners in rural Idaho who support individuals living with chronic health conditions and may benefit from improved provider engagement and awareness of caregiver resources.
Applicants are encouraged to demonstrate how proposed activities will strengthen workforce capacity, improve access to evidence-based prevention and treatment practices, and improve health outcomes for Idahoans living in rural and frontier communities.

Section 3: Award Information
Funding for initiatives related to the Rural Health Transformation Program (RHTP) is appropriated annually through the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services and distributed to all 50 states and U.S. territories. Funding is allocated using a formula that considers population characteristics, with the goal of supporting rural communities where the need for access to healthcare (dental, physical, and mental and behavioral health) is the greatest. RHTP funds are intended to help rural populations within states expand community outreach and support for individuals with mental and behavioral health care needs to community-based access.  

The Idaho Department of Health and Welfare, Rural Health Transformation Program in cooperation with the Division of Behavioral Health anticipates awarding up to fifteen (15) school districts with a maximum of five-year subgrants under this RHTP funding opportunity. The annual award amount is variable depending on the number of awards granted and ranges from eighty thousand ($80,000.00) to one hundred twenty thousand ($120,000.00). Renewal of the subgrant may be considered for up to five (5) total years, subject to federal funding availability, satisfactory program performance, and continued authorization of the Rural Health Transformation Program initiatives within the State of Idaho. Future year funding is not guaranteed and will be determined annually.  

Funding is contingent upon continued availability of federal/state appropriations and programmatic authorization for the Rural Health Transformation Program initiative within the State of Idaho. The Department reserves the right to adjust/modify award amounts or funding requirements, if there are changes in federal funding levels, state legislative appropriations, program performance, grantee is unable to maintain satisfactory performance in accordance with subaward expectations, or the grantee’s capacity to successfully deliver SBFSH’s services.

The Rural Health Transformation Program (RHTP) award number RHTCMS332084 is administered by the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services under the Assistance Listing Number (ALN) 93.798 and is authorized under Section 71401 of Public Law 119-21 of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. 

Detailed information regarding allowable expenses, budget expectations, and financial reporting requirements is provided in the Budget section of this document.  Applicants should develop budgets that align with the program objectives and comply with applicable federal and state financial guidance. Programmatic and fiscal reporting requirements will begin at that start of service implementation and will remain in effect throughout the award period. 
Please note: The subgrant becomes effective upon execution, after all required signatures have been obtained. Consequently, funded activities may not commence until the subgrant is fully executed.

4.2 Project Narrative Guidance
The Project Narrative is the primary basis for evaluating the applicant’s approach to developing a Statewide Chronic Disease Education Network. Address each area below in the order listed. Reviewers will evaluate narrative quality, specificity, and alignment with program goals. Vague or generic responses will score poorly.
A. Statement of Need (recommended: 2–3 pages)
Describe the need for improved access to evidence-based education, training, and workforce development related to chronic disease prevention and treatment across the geographic area proposed for implementation. Applicants should describe the burden of chronic disease in Idaho, including cardiovascular disease, stroke, diabetes, cancer, Alzheimer's disease and other dementias, obesity and metabolic conditions, neurodevelopmental disorders, and immune or allergic conditions, as applicable to the proposed project.
Applicants should identify workforce challenges, gaps in access to specialty expertise, barriers to continuing education, and limitations in existing training and technical assistance opportunities, particularly in rural and frontier communities. Explain how the proposed statewide or multi-regional implementation model will address identified needs and strengthen workforce capacity through partnerships, subawards, learning collaboratives, technical assistance, continuing education opportunities, or other educational approaches. Cite all relevant data sources.
B. Goals, Objectives & Measurable Outcomes (recommended: 2 pages)
State proposed program goals and SMART objectives (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound) that align with improving workforce knowledge, clinical practice, and implementation of evidence-based chronic disease prevention and treatment approaches that are expected to contribute to improved chronic disease outcomes over time.
Applicants should identify both implementation-level outcomes (such as participating organizations, educational activities delivered, workforce participation, continuing education opportunities, geographic reach, or learning collaboratives established) and workforce-level outcomes (such as changes in knowledge, confidence, practice patterns, referral practices, caregiver engagement practices, or adoption of evidence-based approaches). Where feasible, applicants may also describe how these activities are expected to contribute to longer-term improvements in patient or population health outcomes and how those outcomes could be monitored over time.
For each objective, applicants must provide a baseline value, target value, data source, and timeframe for measurement.
C. Approach & Methods (recommended: 4–5 pages)
Describe the proposed implementation model in detail. Explain how the applicant will administer, coordinate, and support a statewide or multi-regional network of participating organizations through subawards, partnerships, learning collaboratives, continuing education opportunities, technical assistance, communities of practice, tele-mentoring, or other workforce development strategies.
Describe the educational approaches, training models, evidence base, subject matter expertise, and implementation supports that will be used. Applicants should explain how activities will be adapted for rural and frontier healthcare settings and how program quality, consistency, accountability, and fidelity will be maintained across participating organizations.
Applicants should describe how education related to prevention, screening, treatment, care coordination, chronic disease self-management, caregiver engagement, and evidence-based clinical practice will be incorporated into workforce development activities when appropriate.
D. Target Population & Engagement (recommended: 1–2 pages)
Describe the workforce populations that will be served through the proposed project, including allied and clinical healthcare providers, healthcare students, nurses, pharmacists, behavioral health professionals, social workers, community health workers, care coordinators, public health professionals, and other members of the healthcare workforce serving rural and frontier communities.
Applicants should describe how workforce training needs were identified and explain strategies for engaging healthcare organizations, academic institutions, professional associations, subject matter experts, family caregivers, community partners, and other stakeholders in planning, implementation, and continuous improvement activities.
If proposing a subgrant model, describe how local needs and stakeholder input will inform subaward decisions and implementation priorities.
E. Partnerships & Collaboration (recommended: 1–2 pages)
Describe key partner organizations and their roles in supporting administration, coordination, workforce education, technical assistance, subject matter expertise, training delivery, workforce development, and sustainability activities.
Applicants should explain how participating organizations, subgrantees, healthcare systems, academic institutions, professional associations, public health organizations, and other stakeholders will contribute to implementation of the project. If proposing a subgrant model, describe the process for selecting, supporting, monitoring, and coordinating participating organizations.
Explain how partnerships will improve statewide reach, reduce duplication, strengthen workforce capacity, and support long-term sustainability. Letters of support, memoranda of understanding, or other partnership agreements may be included as attachments.
F. Evaluation Plan (recommended: 2 pages)
Describe how the project will be evaluated for both implementation and outcomes. Applicants should identify process measures, outcome measures, data collection methods, reporting frequency, and responsible staff.
Applicants should describe how participation, educational activities, workforce development outcomes, implementation efforts, and performance measures will be collected, aggregated, monitored, and reported across participating organizations. Measures may include participation levels, geographic reach, continuing education activities, workforce knowledge gains, implementation of evidence-based practices, caregiver engagement practices, referral practices, and other indicators relevant to project goals.
Explain how evaluation findings will be used to support continuous quality improvement and demonstrate the applicant's capacity to collect, manage, analyze, and report required performance data to the Department.
G. Sustainability (recommended: 1 page)
Describe how workforce education activities, partnerships, implementation structures, and educational resources will be sustained beyond the funding period. Applicants should explain how successful training models, learning collaboratives, technical assistance resources, continuing education opportunities, caregiver engagement content, and evidence-based educational materials will be integrated into ongoing organizational operations or partner networks.
Identify potential future funding sources, organizational commitments, reimbursement opportunities, and partnership strategies that will support continuation of activities. 5.2 Review Process
The review process consists of three stages:
Stage 1 – Administrative Screening: Department staff will screen applications for completeness and eligibility. Incomplete applications or those from ineligible applicants will not advance to technical review. Applicants will be notified of disqualification within 5 business days.
Stage 2 – Technical Review: A review panel of at least three individuals with expertise in chronic disease prevention, public health program management, and federal grants compliance will independently score each application. Reviewers will participate in a consensus scoring session to discuss and finalize scores. Panel members will sign conflict of interest disclosures.
Stage 3 – Final Selection: The Department will make final funding decisions based on panel scores, geographic coverage across the state, population reach, and programmatic priorities. The Department reserves the right to fund applications out of rank order to ensure equitable geographic distribution.
The Department may request clarification, additional information, or interviews from applicants as part of the review process. Funding recommendations will be developed based on final evaluation scores, risk assessment results, available funding, and the Department’s goal of establishing an effective Statewide Chronic Disease Education Network. The Department anticipates funding one (1) Lead Entities capable of administering and overseeing a statewide network of local partners.
Final funding decisions will consider:
Application scores
Funding availability
Geographic coverage
Organizational capacity
Program priorities
Ability to achieve statewide population-level outcomes

6.4 Remedies for Noncompliance

Temporarily withhold payments pending corrective action
Disallow all or part of the cost of the noncompliant activity
Wholly or partly suspend or terminate the subaward
Initiate suspension or debarment proceedings per 2 CFR Part 180
Withhold further awards or impose additional conditions
6.5 Record Retention
Subgrantees must retain all financial records, supporting documents, statistical records, and all other records pertinent to the subaward for a period of six (6) years from the date of submission of the final expenditure report, or as otherwise required by §200.334. If any litigation, claim, or audit is started before the expiration of the retention period, records must be retained until all issues are resolved.
6.6 Procurement Standards
Subgrantees must follow the procurement standards in §§200.318–200.327, including conducting all procurement transactions in a manner providing full and open competition. Subgrantees must maintain written conflict of interest policies and document the procurement process including cost/price analysis.
