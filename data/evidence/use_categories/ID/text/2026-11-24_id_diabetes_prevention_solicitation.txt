Section 1: Program Description & Purpose
Idaho faces significant and persistent disparities in chronic disease outcomes, particularly in rural and frontier communities. According to the Idaho Department of Health and Welfare’s 2025 Burden of Chronic Disease Report, cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, and obesity account for approximately 58% of all preventable deaths statewide.
Diabetes is the 8th leading cause of death among Idahoans, and approximately 145,661 Idaho adults report having diabetes. An estimated 496,749 Idaho adults have prediabetes, with approximately 80% unaware they have the condition. Rural Idahoans are significantly more likely to be diagnosed with diabetes and face additional barriers to prevention, treatment, and self-management services.
Despite the availability of evidence-based interventions, many Idaho communities lack access to National Diabetes Prevention Program (National DPP) services and Diabetes Self-Management Education and Support (DSMES) programs. Existing challenges include workforce shortages, transportation barriers, limited referral pathways, inadequate awareness of available services, and insufficient coordination between clinical and community-based organizations.
The Idaho Department of Health and Welfare seeks to improve diabetes prevention and diabetes self-management outcomes by supporting a statewide coordinating entity that will work collaboratively with local organizations, healthcare providers, public health districts, tribal partners, community-based organizations, and other stakeholders to expand access to National DPP and DSMES services throughout Idaho.
1.1 Background 
The Department will serve as the lead agency for administering the Rural Health Transformation Program (RHT) Grant funding award for the state of Idaho (ID). As such, the Department is committed to removing barriers to treatment, improving health outcomes, and promoting patient-centered care. The RHTP website address is:  https://healthandwelfare.idaho.gov/providers/rural-health-transformation-program-grant/about-rural-health-transformation-program-grant  
As the grant recipient, the Department manages the entire grant award and is responsible for distributing funding to other ID state agencies, contractors, and Subgrantees in alignment with the projects awarded by Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS). This distribution will be carried out in accordance with both state and federal regulation, specifically procurement rules, to facilitate the execution of RHTP work. The state’s RHTP is a critical mechanism to advance a sustainable, data-driven model of care that enhances access, quality, and outcomes.      
Idaho’s Rural Health Transformation Plan (RHTP) (Attachment A, ID RHTP Project Narrative) is a multi-year strategic plan including five (5) major initiatives designed to strengthen rural health systems, increase access to care, enhance quality outcomes, expand workforce capacity, and promote financial sustainability. These initiatives address CMS’ strategic goals for this grant to transform healthcare for residents in the state’s rural areas.

This plan will work to modernize electronic and logistical infrastructure, strengthen community-to-clinician connections, and further integrate healthcare and community-based services across settings and types of care.

1.3 Program Goals & Objectives
Purpose:
The Idaho Department of Health and Welfare (Department) seeks to award up to two (2) Lead subgrantees to establish, administer, and oversee a statewide Diabetes Prevention and Self-Management Network. The successful applicant(s) will serve as pass-through organizations responsible for issuing and managing subgrants to local implementation partners to deliver National Diabetes Prevention Program (DPP) and Diabetes Self-Management Education and Support (DSMES) services throughout Idaho.
The purpose of this funding opportunity is to support a statewide network model in which lead entities administer subgrants to local implementation partners to increase access to evidence-based diabetes prevention and management services, particularly in rural, frontier, and underserved communities, through a coordinated statewide network of local providers.
Required activities:
Administer a statewide network of local implementation partners.
Applicants must allocate most of the requested funding to local implementation partner subgrants.
Conduct competitive subgrant processes to identify and fund local organizations. 
Provide technical assistance, training, and capacity-building support. 
Monitor programmatic and fiscal performance of local subgrantees.
Coordinate statewide outreach, referral, and partnership activities.
Collect, validate, and report performance data.
Ensure compliance with federal, state, and Department requirements.
Support long-term sustainability of diabetes prevention and management services.
Program Goals
Goal 1: Expand access to CDC-recognized National Diabetes Prevention Programs in underserved communities.
Goal 2: Increase access to American Diabetes Association (ADA)-recognized or Association of Diabetes Care & Education Specialists (ADCES)-accredited Diabetes Self-Management Education and Support services.
Goal 3: Strengthen clinical-to-community referral systems connecting healthcare providers with community-based diabetes prevention and management resources.
Goal 4: Improve participant enrollment, retention, completion, and health outcomes through coordinated statewide implementation.
Goal 5: Build sustainable community capacity for diabetes prevention and chronic disease self-management.
Statewide Objectives
Through the network of funded implementation partners, successful applicants will support activities that collectively:
Increase the number of DPP and DSMES delivery sites statewide.
Increase participation among individuals with prediabetes and diabetes. 
Improve access for rural, frontier, Hispanic/Latino, American Indian/Alaska Native, and other underserved populations.
Increase referrals from healthcare providers to community-based services.
Improve participant retention and completion rates.
Reduce the number of Idahoans with Type II diabetes.
Reduce Emergency Room visits due to diabetes.
Strengthen local organizational capacity to sustain services beyond the grant period.
1.4 Scope of Work
The applicant must complete the Non-Duplication Assessment (Appendix H) and submit with application to ensure funds do not support an existing project or plan. 
The Department anticipates awarding up to two (2) Lead Subgrantees that will establish and manage a statewide Diabetes Prevention and Self-Management Network and serve as a pass-through organization responsible for issuing and managing subgrants with local partners for implementation.  Through subgrants, the funded recipient will fulfill the following approved activities:
Network Administration
Develop and administer a statewide network of local implementation partners delivering DPP and DSMES services.
Develop and implement a transparent, competitive process for soliciting, selecting, and awarding subgrants to local organizations.
Execute and manage subgrant agreements with local implementation partners.
Serve as the pass-through entity responsible for compliance with all applicable federal and state requirements.
Technical Assistance and Capacity Building
Provide technical assistance, training, coaching, and ongoing support to funded implementation partners.
Provide technical assistance to local organizations seeking CDC recognition for DPP programs and ADA recognition or ADCES accreditation for DSMES programs.
Develop statewide tools, resources, guidance documents, and best practices to support program implementation.
Coordinate peer-learning opportunities and communities of practice among funded partners.
Program Implementation Oversight
Ensure implementation partners in recruiting, enrolling, and retaining participants.
Ensure development of clinical referral pathways and community partnerships.
Coordinate statewide marketing, outreach, and participant engagement activities.
Promote culturally responsive and community-informed service delivery strategies.
Fiscal and Programmatic Monitoring
Conduct pre-award risk assessments of local implementation partners.
Monitor subgrantee fiscal performance, program performance, and compliance with all applicable requirements.
Conduct desk reviews, monitoring visits, corrective action activities, and follow-up reviews as necessary.
Review and approve reimbursement requests submitted by local implementation partners.
Ensure all subgrantees maintain adequate documentation and comply with applicable federal requirements.
Evaluation and Reporting
The successful applicant must meet at least quarterly with the Department’s Rural Health Transformation Program Data Analytics and Evaluation Contractor to complete the following tasks:  
Develop theory of change diagram(s) (e.g., logic model, line-of-sight diagram, driver diagram, or similar visualization of assumptions connecting proposed work with intended outcomes and performance indicators) 
Use data sharing mechanisms (to be developed by the Data Analytics and Evaluation contractor) to report on required key performance objectives (KPO) within timelines established by the Department which may include, but are not limited to: 
Number of sites implementing new or enhanced evidence-based prevention or treatment programs 
Number of individuals enrolling in a new or enhanced evidence-based prevention or treatment program 
Number of individuals reaching program specific retention or completion milestone

Review and provide input on draft evaluation findings, reports, and analyses as requested

1.5 Target Population & Service Area
Target population: 
The purpose of this initiative is to improve diabetes prevention, self-management, and health outcomes among Idaho residents at risk for or living with diabetes, with a particular focus on populations experiencing disproportionate disease burden, barriers to care, or limited access to evidence-based services.
Lead Subgrantees must ensure equitable access to services through their network of implementation partners and prioritize the following populations:
Priority Population 1: Individuals at Risk for Type 2 Diabetes
Adults aged 18 years and older with prediabetes
Individuals identified as high risk for Type 2 diabetes through clinical screening or validated risk assessment tools
Individuals referred through healthcare providers, employers, health plans, community organizations, or public health programs
Priority Population 2: Individuals Living with Diabetes
Individuals diagnosed with Type 1 diabetes
Individuals diagnosed with Type 2 diabetes
Individuals newly diagnosed with diabetes
Individuals experiencing challenges with diabetes self-management
Individuals at risk for diabetes-related complications
Priority Population 3: Rural and Frontier Communities
Residents of rural and frontier communities
Communities with limited access to DPP or DSMES services
Areas with documented diabetes disparities or shortages of diabetes prevention and management resources
Priority Population 4: Rural Populations Experiencing Access to Care Challenges
Low-income populations
Older adults
Uninsured and underinsured individuals
Medicaid beneficiaries
Hispanic and Latino populations
American Indian and Alaska Native communities
Other populations experiencing disproportionate diabetes burden or barriers to care in rural Idaho communities
Service Area
Eligible service areas are rural and frontier counties within the state of Idaho, as defined by state rural health criteria and federal rural-urban classification systems, including non-metropolitan counties and rural census tracts designated under USDA Rural-Urban Continuum Codes (RUCC) or the Federal Office of Rural Health Policy (FORHP) eligibility criteria. See Appendices A and B for the RHTP eligibility map and a list of eligible and ineligible cities by county.
Lead Subgrantees must develop a sub-granting strategy prioritizing communities with the greatest demonstrated need and the greatest potential for improving diabetes prevention and management outcomes.

5.2 Review Process
Applications will be reviewed by a committee of at least three Department reviewers.
The review process will include:
Administrative Screening: Department staff will screen applications for completeness and eligibility. Incomplete applications or those from ineligible organizations will not advance to technical review. Applicants will be notified of disqualification within 5 business days.

Technical Review: A review panel of at least three individuals with expertise in chronic disease prevention, public health program management, and federal grants compliance will independently score each application. Reviewers will participate in a consensus scoring session to discuss and finalize scores. Panel members will sign conflict of interest disclosures.

Final Selection: The Department will make final funding decisions based on panel scores, geographic coverage across the state, population reach, and programmatic priorities. The Department reserves the right to fund applications out of rank order to ensure equitable geographic distribution. 

The Department may request clarification, additional information, or interviews from applicants as part of the review process. Funding recommendations will be developed based on final evaluation scores, risk assessment results, available funding, and the Department’s goal of establishing an effective statewide Diabetes Prevention and Self-Management Network. The Department anticipates funding one (1) to two (2) Lead Entities capable of administering and overseeing a statewide network of local implementation partners. 
Final funding decisions will consider:
Application scores
Funding availability
Geographic coverage
Organizational capacity
Program priorities
Ability to achieve statewide population-level outcomes

The Department reserves the right to make fewer awards, make partial awards, negotiate budgets, or decline to make any awards.

6.4 Remedies for Noncompliance
If a Subgrantee fails to comply with the terms of the subaward or applicable federal requirements, the pass-through entity may take one or more of the following actions per §200.339:
Temporarily withhold payments pending corrective action
Disallow all or part of the cost of the noncompliant activity
Wholly or partly suspend or terminate the subaward
Initiate suspension or debarment proceedings per 2 CFR Part 180
Withhold further awards or impose additional conditions
6.5 Record Retention
Subgrantees must retain all financial records, supporting documents, statistical records, and all other records pertinent to the subaward for a period of six (6) from the date of submission of the final expenditure report, or as otherwise required by §200.334. If any litigation, claim, or audit is started before the expiration of the retention period, records must be retained until all issues are resolved.
6.6 Procurement Standards
Subgrantees must follow the procurement standards in §§200.318–200.327, including conducting all procurement transactions in a manner providing full and open competition. Subgrantees must maintain written conflict of interest policies and document the procurement process including cost/price analysis.
