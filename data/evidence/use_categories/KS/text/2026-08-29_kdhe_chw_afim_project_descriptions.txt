                 Kansas Rural Health Transformation Program
Community Health Worker (CHW) + Accountable Food is Medicine (AFIM) Awarded
                            Project Descriptions

•   Hutchinson Regional Medical Center, Inc: $150,000
       o Implement an integrated rural health model that connects high-risk
          patients in Reno County with CHW support, nutrition-based interventions,
          chronic disease monitoring, and community resource navigation. Includes
          linking support services like WIC and partnering with K-State Extension.
•   Hospital District No. 6 of Harper County, Kansas: $150,000
       o Launch a Community Health Worker and Accountable Food is Medicine
          (CHW+AFIM) program targeting type 2 diabetes through whole-person,
          patient-centered lifestyle medicine, with the registered dietician-designed
          nutrition (AFIM) intervention and the CHW at its center. Pair food-as-
          medicine with CHW-delivered lifestyle-medicine coaching so the CHW
          team can remove the real barriers to healthier choices.
•   Great Plains of Smith County, Inc: $150,000
       o Implement a fully integrated Community Health Worker (CHW) +
          Accountable Food Is Medicine (AFIM) model to improve outcomes for
          rural Kansans with prediabetes, Type 2 diabetes, gestational diabetes,
          and hypertension. With SCMH’s project, the CHW is embedded within
          existing clinical workflows and the model is uniquely strengthened by a
          dual-trained Registered Dietitian/Executive Chef, enabling direct
          translation of clinical nutrition guidance into practical, culturally appropriate
          food interventions.
•   Hospital District No. 1 of Dickinson County, Kansas, DBA Memorial Health
    System: $150,000
       o Implement the Healthy Dickinson County Initiative, a comprehensive
          program designed to improve outcomes for patients with diabetes and
          hypertension by integrating CHWs, medically tailored meals, social
          determinants of health (SDOH) screening and community resource
          navigation. Memorial Health System will leverage its Food and Nutrition
          Services Department and collaborate with a registered dietician to develop
          and produce medically tailored meals designed specifically for patients
          with diabetes, hypertension, cardiovascular disease, and other nutrition-
          sensitive conditions.
•   CommonSpirit Kansas, Inc: $149,960
       o Implement a comprehensive Community Health Worker (CHW) and
          Accountable Food is Medicine (AFIM) program across Western Kansas.
          Embed CHWs within multidisciplinary clinical teams who will identify, track,
           and close social and structural barriers to health. Establish robust food is
           medicine infrastructure, with inclusion of evidence-based nutrition
           education, local resource navigation, federal benefit enrollment
           assistance, collaboratively created produce prescriptions, and potential
           development of medically tailored groceries and/or meals.
•   Citizens Foundation: $146,476
        o Expand established Community Health Worker (CHW) program and fully
           implement an Accountable Food is Medicine (AFIM) initiative, integrating
           clinical care with community-based support to bridge the gap between
           medical treatment and long-term health management. Leverage on-
           campus food pantry as a clinical entry point and partner with registered
           dietitians through in-person and telehealth consultation to provide
           evidence-based, localized and professionally managed dietary
           interventions. Supported by a robust medical staff of 30 providers,
           including 10 family practice physicians, seven of whom specialize in
           obstetrics, along with two general surgeons, 10 nurse practitioners, and
           six physician assistants.
•   Newman Memorial County Hospital: $110,716
        o Expand established Community Health Worker (CHW) program to include
           a structured, clinically integrated AFIM model with new workflows,
           nutrition-focused interventions, enhanced patient monitoring, expanded
           food access partnerships, cooking and nutrition education initiatives,
           outcome tracking, and sustainable reimbursement mechanisms. This
           includes a robust provider team with dieticians and CHWs integrated into
           workflow and collaboration with existing partners.
